### Added
- **Power calculator: design uncertainty** (the *Board description* list: none, drawn from the real fabrication files, drawn by hand, estimated). The best / nominal / worst band now also moves the copper coverage, the copper weight, the inner planes and the vias of the drawn board, one at a time, and adds them to the other sources by the root of the sum of squares; the amplitudes and the multiplier of each level are in Preferences (`calculator` defaults, `design_*`). The band without them did not cover a wrongly drawn board (three of four drawing errors left the true power above the worst case). The default level is *Drawn by hand*: the example of `docs/calculators.md` 1.1b goes from 2.00 / 1.62 / 2.31 W to 2.00 / 1.56 / 2.34 W (board ring) and from 2.00 / 1.69 / 2.27 W to 2.00 / 1.60 / 2.31 W (top of the package); level *None* reproduces the old band exactly. Amplitudes are limited to 99 % and the best case to 0 W.

- **Thick-plate warning**: after the run, a warning when `q'' t / (2 k)` of the copper layer or of a metal block under a heater exceeds 5 % of the rise of that heater (the surface under the source can be hotter than the model shows by up to that amount; a bound, never added to the result). `q''` is the heat of the part over its footprint for its copper layer and the largest flux that enters the block under the part in the FVM or FDM solution (with only the Lumped, Analytic or 3D solid a quiet auxiliary FVM run at a cell of at least 2 mm supplies it, so the warning is the same as with the FVM) for a block (the footprint flux overstated a block under the board 5 to 70 times and fired on a 3 x 3 mm, 2 W part over 3 mm of steel, where the 3D solid shows no effect). Checked against the 3D solid: the bound is above the real effect of the block thickness in every case. It does not fire on the example boards or on the 74 metal-block cases of the research (median 0.07 %, maximum 0.34 %).
- **Natural convection form** (`Board.natural_form`, Environment panel, Preferences, CLI `--natural-form`, the report): `general` (default, results bit-identical) or the laminar form of Churchill and Chu for a vertical board, with a warning above its range (Ra > 1e9); the general form matches the library `ht`, the laminar one matches the laminar boundary-layer solution within 0.2 % plus its leading-edge constant; the 1975 paper has not been opened; the difference on the example board is -0.7 to +0.2 % of the rise.
- **Independent 3D solver in the tests** (`tests/helpers/layered3d.py`, `tests/test_layered_vs_3d.py`): the five models against it on a laminate coupon and on a one-layer board.
- **Inner copper weight of the typical stack-ups**: the Stack-up tab has an *Inner copper* choice (enabled for an advanced stack-up of 3 or more layers) and `Board.template_inner_oz` (saved in the project only when it is not the default): **0.5 oz (15.2 µm, the JLCPCB standard) stays the default** and reproduces the JLCPCB 4-layer table JLC04161H-7628 (as read on 2026-10-22; its 210.4 µm prepreg was not seen again on 2026-10-24, the core and the inner copper were); **1 oz (35 µm)** is derived from the shipped templates: every inner copper layer 35 µm and **every dielectric of the template unchanged**, so the board is thicker than the nominal by 19.8 µm per inner layer (+0.04 mm for 4 layers to +0.20 mm for 12, 2.5 to 8 %, inside the ±10 % tolerance). Fabricators do not scale the dielectrics when the copper changes, they pick another build; against 15 published 1 oz builds this rule is off by +0.10 K on average (mean absolute 0.85 K) against +0.47 K (1.00 K) for dielectrics scaled to keep the nominal thickness. It is **not a JLCPCB product table** (JLCPCB's own 1 oz inner copper is 30 µm in a third-party collection of its stack-up data); 35 µm is the figure of PCBWay, PCBGOGO, Bittele and AdvancedPCB. Choosing it reloads the typical stack-up as one undoable edit. On the example board with a plane on every inner layer it is 6.9 K (4 layers) to 7.5 K (6 layers) cooler (10.6 to 13.3 % of the rise for the 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12-layer templates; FVM and 3D solid), 0.5 K above the PCBWay and Bittele 4-layer tables and within 0.1 to 0.3 K of the PCBWay, Bittele and PCBGOGO 6-layer tables; with copper only on the outer layers it moves the result by under 0.3 K. `stackup_template(layers, path, inner_oz)`, `docs/validation.md` section 14.1.

### Fixed
- **Side walls of a finned metal block** (found in the review of `research/block-and-fins`): the plate fins stand on the exposed face of the block, but the side walls of the base plate were given the fin ratio too (`h_face·fin_ratio + h_extra` instead of `h_face + h_extra`), in all five models (`blocks.extra_layers`, `solid3d.py`). The rise of a finned block was 5.5 to 6.4 % too low (FVM, 60 × 40 mm board, 40 × 30 × 3 mm aluminium block: 61.2 / 66.4 / 53.2 K became 65.0 / 70.0 / 56.6 K); blocks without fins are bit-identical. `blocks.wall_h` and the key `h_wall` of `extra_layers` are new (not `h_side`: that is the edge coefficient of the board); `h_total` is now the coefficient of the exposed face only. The table of `docs/validation.md` 12 D also still had the Lumped and Analytic columns of the earlier equivalent-coefficient version (100.3 / 99.8 K on the plain plate, now 89.8 / 91.5 K) and a fin ratio of 8.4 for 8.35; both corrected.
- **Top path on small pads** (a heatsink on the package): the split of the power was found by a fixed-point iteration whose slope `-R_b/(R_top + theta)` exceeds 1 when the board under the part resists more than the top path (8 mm pads: 1.3 to 2.4). It oscillated, stopped after 80 iterations and reported the junction 17 to 58 K below the operating point (a 2 W MOSFET: 33.2 degC instead of 90.9) with a misleading "thermal runaway" warning, on the unsafe side. The split is now the root of a monotone scalar equation per part (secant with the Illinois rule; Broyden quasi-Newton for several parts), 6 to 7 board solves on the four parts of the study (more with several coupled parts, and each pass of a power-coefficient loop repeats them); the 25 mm rows of `docs/validation.md` section 9 stay within 0.1 K, boards without a top path are bit-identical, and a split that does not converge says "the top-path split did not converge". The power temperature coefficient alone keeps its loop and its runaway detection. Found by the review of `research/component-heatsink`. `docs/theory.md` section 9, `docs/validation.md` section 9, `tests/test_top_path.py`.
- **A 3D-solid run that never ended** (found as the random-actions stress test timing out after 900 s): the 3D solid accepted up to 2 million unknowns, but its direct sparse solve fills in far more than the layered models (memory measured: 2.2 GB at 160 000 unknowns, 4.4 GB at 254 000, 8 GB at 512 000; the time is seconds to minutes depending on the memory pressure, and 1 000 000 unknowns did not finish in 25 minutes while paging on a 16 GB machine), and with computed heat transfer coefficients it is repeated 3 to 6 times; a 502.5 mm board at 2 mm (a million unknowns) kept the calculation process busy for hours. The limit is now 250 000 unknowns (`solid3d.MAX_CELLS`), checked before anything is built (`solid3d.unknowns`, `solid3d.check_size`); the message gives the cell to use, the other models still answer, and the solver panel shows the exact 3D count and says when it will not run. The FVM and FDM keep no limit; the panel says "slow" above a million unknowns (about 4 GB per million). A cancel kills the calculation process when it does not end on SIGTERM within 5 s, so its memory is always freed. The offline studies that need finer 3D grids raise the limit inside `solid3d.size_limit()`.
- **Metal block on a one-layer board**: the block temperature map showed the first laminate node instead of the block (`arrays["block_T"]` took every layer after the board); the table values were right.
- **Validation section 14 (typical stack-ups against fabricators)**: the 6 and 8-layer rows had been solved on boards with no bottom copper (about 21 K hotter); corrected with the two-column table (outer copper only, and a plane on every inner layer) and the corrected ranges (0.1 to 1.5 °C, 0.2 to 2.0 % of the rise). `docs/theory.md` section 1.2 now describes the advanced stack-up.
- **Stack-up templates, note on the fabricator comparison**: `pcbthermal/data/stackups.json` said the maximum temperature differs from the fabricator tables by under 0.5 %. With the outer copper only the difference is 0.1 to 1.5 °C (0.2 to 2.0 % of the rise), and with a plane on every inner layer the 0.5 oz inner copper of the templates is 5 to 8 K (up to 13 % of the rise) hotter than the 1 oz inner copper of other fabricators' tables, so the templates are the conservative choice. Text only, no values changed.
- **One-layer boards in the layered models**: the laminate under the copper is now two node layers in the FVM, FDM and Lumped (`pcbthermal/laminate.py`), and a series resistance under the bottom film in the Analytic; the computed coefficients take the bottom face from the last laminate node. Against an independent 3D solver the FVM goes from −1.9 % to 0.0 % with full copper and from −10 % to −2.5 % with a bare 20 mm pad; on nine boards closer to real ones its mean error against the 3D solid goes from 3.8 to 1.0 % (one-layer boards: 6.5 to 1.4 %). `docs/validation.md` section 21.
- **Lumped: the ring count**: rings narrower than a cell are merged, so the result no longer grows with the number of rings (40 rings: up to +7 % before, +0.4 % now on the coupon of section 21) or jumps with the source size; the default of 10 rings moves the documented scenarios by at most 0.07 K.
- **Analytic heat balance with a metal block**: the heat leaving the face under a block was counted although the block hides that face (the temperatures were right, the reported balance was about 9 % off); it is now taken off the face total and the balance closes to 1e-13.
- **Boards with an outline: time constant, step responses and transient mean** (FVM, FDM). The cells off the board are pinned to ambient with 1 W/K, and that pin was counted as a path to the air: a round board had a time constant of 0.02 s instead of about 107 s, so the step responses used by the power profiles stopped at 12 % of the steady resistance and the animation length offered was about one second; the transient mean counted the cells off the board at the ambient (46.8 instead of 51.7 °C). `docs/validation.md` section 20.
- **Boards with an outline: part of the edge loss was dropped** (FVM and 3D solid). `geometry.edge_length_per_cell` gave a sample on a cell border to the cell above or to the right, and a side with the board below or to its left was given to a cell off the board, whose edge loss is dropped: a 60 mm T board counted 170 of its 240 mm of perimeter (210 mm upside down), its time constant read 106.72 s against 104.85 s in closed form, and the T and its mirror image differed by 0.31 K in the FVM and the 3D solid (the FDM was not affected). Every sample is now nudged a thousandth of a cell towards the inside of the board: the perimeter, the time constant (104.85 s) and the maximum (93.009 °C FVM, 93.393 °C 3D solid) are the same for both. A second loss of the same kind is corrected with it: a slanted or curved side crosses cells with a sliver of board that no cover sample reaches, those cells are off the board, and the edge through them was dropped too, 2 to 6 % of the perimeter of a disc, an ellipse or a turned square at every cell size (FVM and 3D solid). That length now goes to the nearest cell on the board (`geometry.edge_length_on_board`), so the counted edge is the perimeter of the polygon: an 80 mm round board with an edge film of 100 W/m²K had a time constant of 85.23 s against 84.51 s in closed form and a mean rise 0.64 % above an independent radial solution, now 84.52 s and 0.02 %; a square turned by 3 degrees read 0.98 K hotter than the same square on the grid, now 0.09 K. Boards without an outline and the example boards do not change; the outline table of `docs/validation.md` section 13 moves by at most 0.2 points (L and ellipse rows). Found by the review of `research/outline`. `docs/validation.md` sections 13 and 20.
- **3D solid on a one-layer board**: the laminate under the copper was missing (the board was only its copper), so a 20 mm pad read 120 °C against about 74 °C in the other models; the laminate is now four FR-4 slabs, and an independent 3D solver with patterned copper puts the 3D solid within 0.2 % on a 20 mm pad (it was 40 % high).
- **Power temperature coefficient and top path**: a loop that does not settle in 80 iterations (a board close to thermal runaway) now gives a warning instead of returning its last iterate silently.
- Documents: the plate check of the metal block is +0.6 %, −0.8 % and −4.6 % from Seri Lee's article (centre, edge, corner), not +4.5 % (the check placed the source with `round()`, which made it 24 mm); the source is the Electronics Cooling article of Seri Lee (1998).

## [2.3.0] - 2026-10-16

The finite-difference model in time, metal blocks, the Heatsink rename and a 3D solid model.

### Added
- The **FDM now also does the time-dependent work**: power-step animation (Animation tab, `transient --model fdm`, GIF export), step responses and the power-profile time simulation, with the same theta-method as the FVM on the grid points (the hottest point follows the FVM within 0.7 °C on the example; the profile peak is 3 % lower at 2 mm, where the 6 mm heater is three cells wide). With only the FDM selected it also makes the average-or-resolve decision.
- `transient --model fvm|fdm|analytic`.
- **3D solid model** (`solid3d.py`, key `solid3d`, "3D solid (finite volumes), comparison" in the application, `--model solid3d|compare`): the board as a real 3D solid on the plan grid with real slabs (every copper layer, four per dielectric gap, metal blocks as pad and metal slabs, vias with their real copper area); steady state, SciPy only; the number of 3D unknowns is shown and a too large model is skipped with a warning. Within 0.2 % of an independent finite-element solid on three boards and 1.6 % on a 20 mm pad; within 0.3 % of the FVM on ordinary scenarios. Validation tables of `docs/validation.md` now include it.
- `research/component-heatsink/scripts/component_study.py`: what a Component parametrisation adds on five real parts (one resistance to the pad adds 2 to 28 % of the rise, a top path matters only with a heatsink); results and the proposed minimal Component in `docs/roadmap.md` 1.9b.
- `research/solid3d-verification/scripts/copper_dissipation.py` and `tests/test_copper.py`: the outer copper dissipates and spreads heat by itself and a heatsink works through the copper under it (FVM within 2.5 % of a 3D solid for pads of 20 mm and more).
- **Metal block**: a Heatsink with a thickness (material, interface resistance, extra coefficient for fins) is a heat spreader in the FVM and FDM with lateral conduction, mass and a contact only over the copper it overlaps; block results (table, temperature view, report), checks, `example block`; validated against a 3D solid within 1.8 %.

### Changed
- **Sink is now called Heatsink** everywhere (class, `add_heatsink`, `Problem.heatsinks`, JSON key `heatsinks`, application labels, documentation). Project files saved with the key `sinks` and the old editor files still open.

## [2.1.0] - 2026-10-16

Power profiles and computed heat transfer coefficients.

### Added
- **Power profiles**: a heater can have a square wave (period or frequency with unit, duty, power while on and off; the average is derived) instead of a constant power.
  Every heater is either replaced by its average power or simulated in time, decided per heater from the oscillation in °C (default 1 °C) with a shared error budget;
  heaters in both regimes run together. Works in all three models (FVM, Lumped, Analytic modal), start from the steady state of the average power or from a typed
  temperature, run until the cycle repeats / for N cycles / for a fixed time, step cap 200,000. Results: temperature versus time, peak, minimum, oscillation,
  steady bounds with the average and the peak power, peak-temperature map and the last window as frames. Steady models can use the average (default) or peak power.
- GUI: "Power profile" tab (editor, decisions, settings) and "Power profile" results tab; CLI: `run --duration/--cycles/--time-s/--initial-c/--threshold-c/--steady-power`,
  `example --which profile`; `profile_<model>.csv/png` and `peak_map_<model>.png` outputs.
- Model labels in the GUI: "Finite volume (FVM), reference", "Thermal network (LPTN)", "Analytical (Fourier series)"; temperature differences shown in °C (thermal resistance stays in K/W, the same number as °C/W).

- **Computed heat transfer coefficients** (`Board.h_mode = "computed"`): natural and forced convection plus radiation per face from the mean face temperature, orientation, emissivities, air speed and a global calibration multiplier of the convection, solved by iteration (3 to 4 solves) in all three models. GUI: Environment panel; CLI: `run --computed-h --air-speed --orientation --emissivity --h-multiplier`.
- Profile plot: a fast wave is drawn as a band, the hottest-point line is no longer hidden.

### Notes
- The analytic transient uses vias and sinks smeared over the board (a warning quantifies the difference); use the FVM near them.

## [2.0.0] - 2026-10-16

First release of the rebuilt program. The original wxPython application (1.0.0) was replaced by a physics package and a new GUI.

### Added
- `pcbthermal` physics package without GUI dependencies, with a public API (`SolverOptions`, `simulate`, `Problem` builder in mm, project files `.pcbt.json`).
- Three models on one problem description: **FVM** (cell-centred finite volumes, sparse matrices, reference), **Lumped** (offset-contour thermal networks, one per heater)
  and **Analytic** (multilayer cosine series with local via and sink corrections). Guide: `docs/model_guide.md`.
- Exact energy balance in every result, input validity checks (`check_problem`) and model warnings, automatic cell size.
- SOR as an optional FVM solver (vectorised red-black) to compare with the sparse direct solver (default); both give the same temperatures.
- Transient response (implicit Euler, Crank-Nicolson) with real-time animation: GUI Animation tab and `transient --gif`.
- Component junction temperature (`theta_jb`), hotspot list, gradient maps, profiles, zone and heater statistics.
- PySide 6 application: board editor (rectangles, polygons, vias, sinks), undo/redo, inspector, background image, dark and light themes, background simulation,
  results tabs, temperature overlay on the board, export of reports, CSV and figures. The version is shown in the title bar, status bar and About box.
- Command line: `run`, `solvers`, `transient`, `convergence`, `convert`, `gui`, `example`, `--version`.
- 94 tests, including a documentation gate that requires Doxygen-style documentation on every function.
- Documentation: `docs/` (model guide, physics, old-versus-new comparison, findings log, candidate methods from the literature).

### Changed
- FR-4 is anisotropic (0.3 W/mK through the thickness, 0.9 W/mK in the plane); vias are hollow barrels with plating thickness; heater power is injected exactly on any grid.
- Projects use millimetres and 1-based layers; legacy editor JSON files still open.
- Dependencies are installed in a virtual environment (`.venv/`, see `requirements.txt`).

### Removed
- The wxPython editor, the old solvers (`core/`, `ui/`, `main.py`, `config.py`) and the iterations GIF (it animated solver iterations, not time). They remain in the git history.

### Fixed (relative to 1.0.0)
- FDM false convergence (step-size stop rule) and grid-dependent heater power (8.17 W injected for 5.50 W requested on the default grid).
- Dimensionally wrong Analytic formula and an inconsistent Lumped network (errors of several hundred to tens of thousands of degrees).
- FDM import failure without Numba.
Details and evidence: `docs/findings.md`.
