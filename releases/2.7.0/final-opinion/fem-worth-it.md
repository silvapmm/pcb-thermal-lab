# Is a finite-element method worth having? (role A in the app, role B reference only)

Web research plus the local review. No repository edits. Status of each source: R = page fetched and read (through the fetch tool, which returns a model-made digest of the page, not the raw page), S = search snippet only, M = from memory (marked), N = not obtained.

## 0. What the local review says (R, read in full the relevant parts)
the research review of the tree, section 6, risk 1: every "independent" 3D reference in the tree (layered3d, graded3d, ref3d, truth3d, review_rect3d, gap-subdivision) is a Cartesian cell-centred FV with harmonic means, the same family as `solid3d.py`: independent implementations, not independent methods. Conclusions on edge film, cut cells, strong edge and laminate band (validation 32-40) rest on that family. The only structurally different check is the scikit-fem hex FEM of `solid3d-verification/scripts/fem3d_validation.py` (2.3.0 era, not repeated; skfem is not installed in this machine's python3). The review asks for one folder, three canonical edge cases (turned 20 mm square with copper to the wall at h_side 200/1000; 10x10 mm 4-layer strong-edge board with a via 0.16 mm from the wall; 6 mm bare window of laminate-nodes), compared with cached FV references to 0.5 %, 1 to 2 days. The question asked here is exactly that, so role B is already the review's top-ranked investigation.

Pattern: FEM where the geometry is a solid body to mesh conformingly (Celsius solids, COMSOL, Elmer); FV where flow is coupled (Icepak, Flotherm, Celsius fluid) and in the lightweight layout-based tools (KiCad_Thermal_Sim, our program). Cadence's blog (via snippet): FEA mostly for conduction in solids, FVM for flow/flux problems; vendor view, not a benchmark. The Cadence "FEM vs FVM" page itself yielded nothing usable.

## 2. Published FEM vs FVM vs FDM on layered PCBs
- No head-to-head study of FEM, FVM and FDM on a thin copper/FR-4 laminate was found (snippets only; extended search on Robin/cut-cell was also thin). I do not claim one exists or not beyond my searches.
- Electronics Cooling (2017, "problem effective thermal conductivity", R digest): a FEM model of a PCB resolving traces needs "many millions of elements", about 1e6 nodes for 6 layers 100x100 mm with 0.25 mm traces, over 1e7 for 10 layers 250x250 mm; the author prefers finite-difference control volumes built from layer bitmaps (the same idea as our imported-copper path). Volume-weighted conductivity can be off by factors (9 K vs 236 K in an example). This supports: FEM on copper pixels gains nothing, FEM needs polygon-conforming meshes and many elements.
- COMSOL tutorial (R digest): copper as shell or extruded traces; free tetrahedral/swept meshes. The conformity of mesh to the copper outline is the point of the FEM route; the thin-layer shell route removes the 1000:1 aspect ratio in the mesh.
- Cut-cell FV literature (S, snippets): Bochkov-Gibou type cut-cell FV with Robin boundaries are second order in solution; flux-based cut cells cost geometry computation; a Robin formulation reaching second order in cut cells was reported as a gap before the 2021 paper (arXiv 2102.07966, S). Nordbotten 2014 (S): cell-centred FV competitive with FE for deformable porous media, not conduction. So no source found that quantifies "FV vs FEM error at a cooled edge of a thin layered plate". That gap is real and is what a role-B study would fill with numbers of our own.
- General facts (M, textbook): linear FEM is conservative globally (and the flux is conservative in a weak sense per element patch), FV locally; Robin condition is natural in FEM (boundary integral, h*T) and exact on a conforming boundary; with a conforming mesh the edge film is applied on the true boundary with no sliver cells; the price is mesh generation. Contrasts of 1000:1 in k are handled fine by both; aspect ratio of thin copper (18-35 um vs mm cells) needs anisotropic/prismatic or shell elements in FEM, as in our laminate sub-gap problem.

## 3. Cost of FEM inside the app (role A)
Licences: scikit-fem BSD-3 (R, README: "the contents of skfem/ and the PyPI package are BSD-3; some docs examples have different licences"), minimal deps numpy+scipy, optional meshio/matplotlib; has MeshTri/Quad/Tet/Hex, hex-to-tet split, tensor meshes, circle/ball meshes, default solver scipy spsolve (R). Triangle (Shewchuk): free but not for commercial inclusion, Debian non-free (R snippets from the meshpy licence file and Triangle page): do not use; meshpy wrapper MIT but bundles Triangle/TetGen with their terms (S, the meshpy FAQ is inconsistent). Gmsh: GPL-2-or-later with a linking exception (S); whether the exception covers a Python caller is disputed in one 2019 mailing-list opinion (S): treat as not allowed in the app (CLAUDE.md forbids GPL code vendored; pip-installed GPL dependency is a legal question for the maintainer). FEniCS/DOLFINx LGPL (M), heavy (MPI, PETSc). earcut ISC (M, not confirmed). So a permissive, pip-only route to a conforming mesh of an arbitrary polygonal board with holes and copper is the hard part: constrained Delaunay without Triangle/gmsh would have to be written (ear clipping exists already in `geometry.py` for area, but quality meshing with refinement is a project of its own, 5 to 15 days and a robustness tail on hostile Gerber copper: slivers, self-touching polygons).
A hex/voxel FEM on the same Cartesian grid adds nothing structurally: with the same cell geometry it has the same cut-cell coverage problem (the outline is voxelised) and, for a regular grid, trilinear Q1 gives stencils close to the FV/FDM ones. It would be a third copy of the FDM/FVM family. Say so plainly (the 3D solid already is that family in 3D).
Effort (my estimate, not measured): minimal triangle-prism FEM of the layered model (2D conforming triangle mesh of the outline and copper, layers as shell/prism with copper conductance, vias as line couplings, Robin edges) 15 to 25 working days including mesher; plus tests, theory section with figure, tooltips, user guide, validation, frozen numbers (a sixth model in the 17-board harness), memory-guard law, GUI model list and stress scenarios: 10 to 15 more. Total roughly 5 to 8 weeks. Benefits: no cut-cell error on rounded/slanted outlines, resolved via cylinders, a second answer in-app. Risks: sixth model to maintain, mesher robustness on arbitrary imported copper (the program's strength is raster copper, which FEM does not use), speed/memory at 1e6 unknowns (scipy SuperLU, same as ours; tet meshes have more nonzeros per row, about 14 to 15 vs 7), and the user cannot tell which model to trust; the program's 2.6 effort was spent making the FVM treat cut cells correctly (validation 32-40), and a FEM would not retire the need for those.

## 4. Error of cell-centred FV vs FEM at a cooled edge of a thin plate
No quantitative published comparison found (see 2). Local evidence only: validation 32-40 (cut-cell links, film, band), and the unrepeated 2.3.0 skfem check. A role-B study produces this number for our cases.

## 5. Minimal role B: what the folder should build
- Scratch venv (not .venv, not requirements): scikit-fem (BSD), meshio, and for meshing gmsh or triangle (allowed in scratch because nothing is shipped; licence noted in README; nothing vendored).
- Method must differ structurally from cell-centred FV: conforming unstructured mesh. Plan: 2D constrained triangle mesh of the true outline (polygon, turned square exactly rotated), extruded in z to wedge/tet layers per copper and dielectric layer (check that skfem has wedge or use extrude+tet split; to verify, M), P1 elements, Robin edge integral exact on the wall, copper sheet as thin layer (prism with k=400 and real thickness, or shell via 2D triangle elements with conductance; the prism version is the conservative choice for the check). Via as a cylinder is a stretch goal.
- Verification of the FEM itself before use: (i) patch test / 1D series with Robin (exact closed form); (ii) energy balance; (iii) Seri Lee / Muzychka flux channel and Culham coupon from papers/ (reproduce published numbers); (iv) a convective-edge strip with an exact fin-series solution (cosh/sinh) for a thin plate with h_side; (v) disc with Robin edge against radial Bessel solution (the repo already has a radial check). Convergence by mesh refinement, Richardson/GCI on the hottest rise; report P1 vs P2 difference.
- Then the three canonical cases of the review, plus the aligned square (cut = no cut) as a control, with h_side 50/200/1000; compare with cached FV refs (target 0.5 %, report any bias with sign). Include the P1-hexahedral run on the same voxel grid only as a control to show how much the method (not the grid) matters.
- Output: README numbered results, what was read, INTEGRATION.md that says whether any program change follows. Size: 3 to 5 days (the review said 1 to 2; the mesher and Robin/thin-layer set-up are the unknowns).

## 6. Recommendation
- (A) FEM as a sixth in-app model: NO for now (later only if the maintainer wants arbitrary-outline accuracy as a product feature and a permissive mesher exists). Reasons: copper from Gerber is raster, the mesher is the cost and the licence trap, 5 to 8 weeks, a sixth model to freeze and document, and the benefit is mostly the cut-cell/edge region that the 2.6 work addressed.
- (B) FEM as independent reference only (research folder, never shipped): YES, it is the review's top item, it retires or exposes the shared-scheme risk of validation 32-40; 3 to 5 days.
- Decision rule for later: if B shows the FV edge treatment within about 0.5 to 1 % on the three cases, A is not needed; if it finds a bias above 2 %, fix the FV scheme first (cheaper than a sixth model) and re-ask A.
