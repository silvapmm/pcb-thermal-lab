## 2. Findings, ranked

1. **Decision 6 is right, its derivation is not (wrong document, fixed where it is quoted).** The decision keeps 0.664 because '0.742 ... is the coefficient of the boundary-layer term in their composite on the length scale `√A` (their eq. 17, a linearised coefficient fitted to their CFD)' and 'put on `L` alone it is a mixture of two length scales'. On the page (Yovanovich and Teertstra 2001, PDF 3, image): 0.742 comes from their **linearised analytical model with `C_T` = 2.31**, a value they **assume** 'close to various averages' of 2.77 (matched to Pohlhausen's wall flux) and 2.13 (matched to the integral enthalpy flux); what is fitted to the CFD is the interpolation parameter `n` (1.42). Eqs 17 and 18 are written on the flow length `L`; the recast on `√A` (eq. 28, with `Re*_√A = Re_√A √(L/W)`) leaves the boundary-layer term identical. So 0.742 is an approximation of **the same** flat-plate boundary layer, 11.7 % above the exact similarity value 0.664 (Lienhard eq. 6.68, printed p 309; their own eq. 12, 'the most frequently quoted value'). The decision also says the uniform-flux mean is 'about 0.68, within 2.4 %': the book prints 0.688 (eq. 6.72), 3.6 %. The conclusion holds for these reasons: 0.664 is exact for the plate the program models, the board is between isothermal and uniform flux (0.664 to 0.688), and both 0.742 and 0.688 would cool the board. The effect quoted, +5 to +9 %, reproduces (+5.2 to +9.0 % with the bottom face). Theory 8 now carries the corrected sentence; the docstring sentence the decision asks for is program item P1 (the reviewer of L5 does not edit `pcbthermal/` on this branch).
2. **Validation 8 was stale (wrong document, fixed).** Every temperature of its table had moved by 0.1 to 1.7 °C since it was printed (2.6.0 and 2.7.0 model items and the face-down law: the bottom coefficient of the table, 10.1 W/m²K, was the one before `fix/face-down-convection`, whose own sentence below the table says 10.38). Re-run and replaced; the reading 'a constant `h` over-estimates the rise by 10 % at 22 W' becomes 11 % (241.9 against 218.6 K). 'Air properties agree with textbook values within 1 to 3 %' is replaced by the measured comparison with Table A.6.
3. **The uncertainty of `h` was given without a source and attributed to the hot spots (misleading, fixed in `docs/models.md` 5 and 18; the user guide is P4).** '20 to 30 % on a board with hot spots' has no source. The printed spread is that of the plate laws themselves: Lewandowski's review as printed by Yovanovich and Jafarpur (1993, p 8): vertical plates ±20 %, horizontal faces up ±50 %. Applying the laws at the mean face temperature instead of cell by cell changes the heat a face loses by under 1 % on the example board (radiation 0.79 %, natural convection 0.81 % on the top face), on the hot side. So the hot spots are not the uncertainty; the laws are.
4. **The turbulent branch of the face up is 7 % above the printed second source, and steps at 10⁷ (limit, documented).** `0.15 Ra^{1/3}` is from memory; Lienhard eq. 8.35 prints 0.14 (with a Prandtl factor 1.0005 at 0.71). At `Ra` 10⁷ the program jumps from 30.37 to 32.32 (+6.4 %). A board face reaches 10⁷ only with `A/P` above about 0.15 m at a 30 K rise (a 0.6 m square), so no PCB is affected. Theory 8 and models 18 say so; no program change proposed (P5 optional).
5. **`L = A/P` on a concave outline is outside the planforms of the source (limit, measured).** Lloyd and Moran tested discs, squares, rectangles and right triangles (YJ p 7); the program takes the perimeter of the outline itself. With the convex-hull perimeter the rise of the two concave harness boards moves by +0.63 % and +1.16 %: small, unsafe direction for the program's choice. Recorded in models 18; a maintainer's question (D2).
6. **The mixed blend exponent (cannot be checked from its source; second source differs, limit).** Churchill (1977) is not in the library. Jaffer and Jaffer (2025, rough plates) use `p` = 2 face up and vertical-in-level-flow, `p` = 5 face down; with equal terms the program's `p` = 3 gives a face-up convection 11 % below and a face-down one 10 % above theirs (top hotter, bottom cooler), which agrees with `research/theory-sources` 3.4 (top 7 to 11 % lower, bottom 0.5 to 8 % higher). Lienhard warns that opposing flow degrades heat transfer; the program always adds. Recorded in theory 8.
7. **No warning on the Reynolds number (limit, program item P3).** The laminar plate holds for about 100 < Re < 10⁵ (YT eq. 12; Lienhard p 309: transition 'typically over 10⁵', 4·10⁴ to 10⁵ with disturbances, p 332). The program stays laminar to 5·10⁵ and has no lower bound: both errors under-state `h` (hot side). At `Re` 1.4·10⁵ (150 mm at 15 m/s) a board with parts is likely turbulent. The mixed branch above 5·10⁵ is Lienhard's abrupt-transition Example 6.11 with `Re_t` 5·10⁵ and `Pr^{1/3}` in place of `Pr^{0.6}` (+9.6 %), continuous at 5·10⁵ (469.5 against 469.8); unreachable on boards.
8. **The face-down constant: the length is read, not printed (narrower statement, fixed in theory 8).** YJ p 8 states `L = A/P` in its paragraph on the faces up; the face-down paragraph (0.44 to 0.527, the average 0.475) does not repeat the length. The 0.475 itself and its range are confirmed on the image.
9. **The air fits beyond 400 K (narrower statement).** Within 2.2 % from 250 to 400 K as documented; at 450 K, inside the '250 to 450 K' of the docstring, Pr is −3.5 % and α +2.4 % because `c_p` is held at 1007 (table 1021). Negligible for `h` (film temperatures of boards stay below 400 K); theory 8 says so; the docstring range is P2.
10. **The agreement of Example 8.1 is two small offsets (explanation tested).** The program's `Nu` is 47.21 (book 47.33, −0.26 %, from its own `Ra` 6.72·10⁷) and its `k` is 0.67 % above the book's, so `h` 4.233 against 4.22 (+0.3 %). With the book's properties the coded formula gives the printed `Nu` 47.33 exactly: the formula is right and the residue is the air fit. Example 6.5: 21.388 (book properties 21.383, printed 21.4).

## 6. What could not be checked, and what to download

* **Churchill and Chu (1975)** (*Int. J. Heat Mass Transfer* 18, 1323-1329): the forms are checked through Lienhard 8.13a/b (R), which is enough for the code; the paper would settle where 0.68 comes from.
* **Churchill (1977)**, *A comprehensive correlating equation for laminar, assisting, forced and free convection*, AIChE J. 23 (1), 10-16: the source of the blend exponent 3. Not checkable here; second source (Jaffer and Jaffer) differs.
* **Lloyd and Moran (1974)**, J. Heat Transfer 96 (4), 443-447: the up-face law, its planforms and its range are read through YJ p 7 (R); the paper itself (mass transfer at `Sc` 2300) would say whether the shapes were all convex.
* **Goldstein and Lau (1983)**, J. Fluid Mech. 129, 55-75: the face-down constants and the length they are on.
* **Raithby and Hollands**, *Natural convection*, Handbook of Heat Transfer (Rohsenow et al.), chapter 4: the source of Lienhard 8.35 and 8.37.
* **Incropera and DeWitt** (any edition after the 6th): the 0.52 Ra^{1/5} face-down form and the 0.15 Ra^{1/3} form; not in the library.
* **The US Standard Atmosphere 1976** (the Sutherland constants 1.458·10⁻⁶ and 110.4, and the conductivity form with 245.4·10^{−12/T}): checked here only against Table A.6.
* Not re-run: the 2D plate solves of `research/plate-h-level` and `research/horizontal-plate-h` (they are the program's own numerical references; A1 re-ran their closed forms); a mixed-convection case (none exists); a measured board (none exists).

## 9. Verdict

**GO for the trust of the convection chapter, with the corrections of this branch.** Every coded formula of L1 and L5 was compared with a printed page read as an image (or, for the blend, Churchill 1977 and the papers not in the library, said not checkable) and none is coded differently from its source: the vertical forms, the forced mean, the face-down constant, the radiation and the Rayleigh definition are the printed ones, and both worked examples of Lienhard reproduce exactly when the book's air properties are put in. What was wrong was documentary: the derivation of decision 6 (0.742 is an assumed linearised constant, not a CFD fit or another length scale), a stale validation table, an uncertainty quoted without a source and blamed on the hot spots, the turbulent up-face branch credited to no printed source, and two page references. The limits that remain are those of the laws themselves (no measured copper board, the up-face law below its range, the blend exponent from a summary, concave outlines, no Reynolds warning); they are written down with their measured size.
