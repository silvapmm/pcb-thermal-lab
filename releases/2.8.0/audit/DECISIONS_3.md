# Decisions on the heat transfer coefficients of the 2.8.0 audit, third set

The maintainer asked whether the heat transfer coefficients of the program are correct, to correct them and to bring `docs/theory.md` 8 in line. This file is the reviewer's answer, law by law, with the decisions that follow; the numbering continues that of `DECISIONS_2.md` (decisions 12 to 33). Program: `development` abc43bae (2.7.1 plus the merged fix and audit branches, including `fix/convection-warnings`). Inputs: `REVIEW_L1L5.md`, `DECISIONS.md` 6 and 8, `DECISIONS_2.md` 12 to 14 and 30, `pcbthermal/convection.py`, `api._solve_computed_h`, the defaults of a new board (`pcbthermal/data/defaults.json`, `model.Board`). Nothing in `pcbthermal/` or `tests/` is edited on this branch (`docs/h-laws-theory`); the theory text of section 8 and the film-term paragraph of section 1.2 are rewritten on it as text of what is implemented now, with the planned changes marked.

**Pages read on the image for this file** (papers library, read only, rendered with pypdfium2): [Lienhard2024] printed pp 435, 436 (PDF 449, 450: eqs 8.35, 8.36, 8.37a/b, the stable cases and the blocked edge circulation) and p 333 (PDF 347: eq. 6.117, which is Churchill's blend of the laminar, transitional and turbulent **forced** terms, not a free-plus-forced blend: the source map's pointer to it for the mixed exponent is wrong); [YovanovichJafarpur1993] pp 7 and 8 (eq. 15 to 17, the Goldstein-Lau face-up constants 0.560 to 0.688 and strip 0.621, Lewandowski's air correlations `0.612 Ra^{1/4}` vertical and `0.766 Ra^{1/5}` horizontal and his ±20 / ±45 / ±50 % review, the face-down constants 0.44 to 0.527 and the average 0.475).

**Measurements** (scratch scripts of the review, light, one job at a time, `PCBTHERMAL_CONFIG` private): (1) the closed forms of every law at `Pr` 0.71 over `Ra` 10² to 2·10⁷ (the table of `docs/theory.md` 8); (2) the 17 boards of the frozen-number harness (`releases/regression-harness/lib/regress_boards.py`) turned to computed `h`, horizontal, laminar form, FVM at 2 mm, with the law of the program and with each alternative patched in; (3) the coefficients the laws give for a new-board-sized plate (`convection.board_h`) and four boards at manual 10 / 15, 15 / 10 and computed coefficients. The figure `research/theory/figures/fig_48_h_laws.png` (copied to `docs/images/`) is drawn by `research/theory/scripts/make_fig_h_laws.py`.

## 34. The face-up law `0.54 Ra^{1/4}`: keep

**Question.** The audit (`REVIEW_L1L5` F-001, `DECISIONS_2` 12) found the face-up law 9 to 22 % above the Raithby-Hollands law printed by [Lienhard2024] (eq. 8.37) from `Ra` 10⁴ to 10⁷. A higher `h` makes the board read **cooler** (unsafe side). Change it?

**Evidence.** The coded law is the Lloyd-Moran correlation, read as reprinted (eq. 17 of [YovanovichJafarpur1993], p 7, image), `2.2·10⁴ ≤ Ra ≤ 8·10⁶` on `A/P`, a mass-transfer correlation at `Sc` 2300. Raithby-Hollands is not the only printed face-up law, and over the PCB range (`Ra` on `A/P` 6·10¹ to 2·10⁶ for 20 to 300 mm boards at 5 to 80 K) it is the **lowest** of them from about 3·10³ up. Program / law − 1 at 10² / 10³ / 10⁴ / 10⁵ / 10⁶: Raithby-Hollands −13.6 / −0.9 / +8.8 / +15.4 / +19.6 % (8.37b applied at every Ra as the audit did; +25.6 % above `Nu` 10 if 8.37b is applied only where the book says, `Nu ≤ 10`); Goldstein-Sparrow-Jones (`Sc` 2.5, data 1 to 10⁴) −17.4 / −8.5 / −8.5 % (the program lower); Lewandowski's air plate −11.3 / −0.4 / +11.7 / +25.4 / +40.7 %; the upper bound eq. 25 of [YovanovichJafarpur1993] −20.8 / −3.5 / +9.9 / +19.3 / +25.3 %. The experiments reviewed on p 8 span exponents 1/8 to 1/4 and constants 0.38 to 0.96; Lewandowski's review puts the horizontal results at ±50 %. Lienhard's own two laws disagree at 10⁷ by 21 % (8.37 gives 24.9, 8.35 gives 30.2; the program 30.4). Effect of Raithby-Hollands on the face up, 17 harness boards at computed `h`: the hottest rise +0.17 to +1.62 % (example board +1.28 %, 60.07 to 60.84 K; the top coefficient falls by 0.3 to 0.8 W/m²K).

**Decision.** Keep `0.54 Ra^{1/4}`. It lies inside the printed spread at every `Ra` of a board; replacing it by the lowest printed law would be a choice of side, not a correction, and its effect (under 2 % of the rise) is a tenth of the spread of the laws. The direction is stated in the theory (cooler than Raithby-Hollands above `Ra` about 2·10³, hotter than Goldstein-Sparrow-Jones), the warning band of decision 12 stays. The earlier maintainer's decision (CLAUDE.md, 'Convection decision': revisit the horizontal laws with the measurement of board D) is confirmed, not reopened.

**Frozen numbers**: none (no frozen record uses computed `h`). **Risk**: the unsafe side of up to 22 % in `h_c` of the top face (under 2 % of the rise) remains, stated.

## 35. The turbulent branch of the face up: change to [Lienhard2024] eq. 8.35

**Evidence.** `0.15 Ra^{1/3}` above `Ra` 10⁷ is from memory (Incropera, not opened); eq. 8.35 (printed p 435, image) is `0.14 Ra^{1/3}(1 + 0.0107 Pr)/(1 + 0.01 Pr)`, `Ra ≥ 10⁷` to 2·10¹¹, 0.1401 at `Pr` 0.71. The coded branch is 7 % above it and steps +6.4 % at 10⁷ (30.37 to 32.32); with 8.35 the step is −0.6 % (30.37 to 30.18). No board reaches it (`A/P` above about 0.15 m at 30 K): the 17 harness boards at computed `h` move by 0.0000 %.

**Decision.** Change, on `fix/horizontal-face-laws` (specification below): a constant from memory is replaced by the printed one at no cost. **Frozen numbers**: none; **pending file**: none. **Order**: it moves no number, so it is independent of the model corrections of step 3 (`DECISIONS_2` 31); it may go with step 2 (it touches `convection.py` and the guide, after `fix/typical-values-marks` if both touch the guide) or at any time before the refreeze. It does not interact with the film term of decision 8 (the computed-`h` loop reads the node in both).

## 36. The coefficients of a new board (10 / 15 / 10 W/m²K): keep the values, document them; the maintainer may choose otherwise

**Evidence.** `new_board` of `defaults.json` and `model.Board`: manual mode, `h_top` 10, `h_bottom` 15, `h_side` 10 W/m²K. Source: none; they are the defaults of the original program 1.0.0 (`config.py` of `641da87`), and no document said so or why the bottom face has more than the top (now said in theory 8). Against the program's computed coefficients (still air, ε 0.9) a 100 × 80 mm board has top / bottom 12.1 / 9.3 (10 K), 13.6 / 10.1 (20 K), 15.5 / 11.3 W/m²K (40 K): the default sum (25) is within about −10 to +15 % of the computed sum, the split is the reverse of the laws. Hottest rise, FVM 2 mm: example board 10 / 15 +0.4 % against computed, 15 / 10 −0.7 %; two hot single-part plates (130 to 140 K, where computed coefficients have grown) +7.2 % and +8.8 % with 10 / 15, +5.2 % and +7.2 % with 15 / 10; a one-layer pad −2.7 % and −4.6 %. The edge 10 W/m²K is about a third of the vertical law plus radiation on a 1.6 mm edge (23 to 28 + 6 to 7 W/m²K, at `Ra` 4 to 12, outside any correlation); 30 instead of 10 reads 1.1 to 1.5 % of the rise cooler: the default edge is on the hot side.

**Decision.** Keep the values (they move nothing that exists: every saved project stores its coefficients, and the frozen boards set theirs); the theory now states their origin, the measured comparison and the direction. **Maintainer**: whether a new board should start with 15 / 10 (the split of the laws; moves only new projects, and the frozen boards built from `model.Board` defaults if the class default is changed too, so change only `defaults.json` if this is chosen) or in computed mode. The reviewer's recommendation: keep, because the sum is right within the scatter of the laws and the swap moves the result by under 2 % of the rise.

## 37. The mixed-convection exponent 3: keep

**Evidence.** Churchill (1977) is not in the library (N); [Lienhard2024] prints no free-plus-forced blend (p 441; eq. 6.117, p 333, is the forced laminar-transitional-turbulent blend). [JafferJaffer2025] (rough 305 mm plates in level flow) use 2 face up and 5 face down. At equal terms `p` = 3 is 10.9 % below `p` = 2 (face up: hotter) and 9.7 % above `p` = 5 (face down: cooler). On the 17 harness boards at 1 m/s (computed `h`, FVM 2 mm) Jaffer's exponents read the hottest rise −0.35 to −1.94 % cooler: the program is on the hot side overall.

**Decision.** Keep `p` = 3 and the limit as written (theory 8); revisit with Churchill (1977) or a measurement. **Frozen numbers**: none.

## What was not verified here

* Churchill and Chu (1975), Churchill (1977), Lloyd and Moran (1974), Goldstein and Lau (1983), Raithby and Hollands (Handbook) and Incropera: not in the library; every statement about them rests on the reprints named above.
* The Goldstein-Sparrow-Jones law is a mass-transfer law (`Sc` 2.5) with data to `Ra` 10⁴; the table extends it beyond for comparison only (marked in brackets).
* No law was compared with a measured copper board; the effect sizes are the program's own FVM at 2 mm on the harness boards turned to computed `h`, not a reference.
* The vertical boards were not re-run with the alternatives (the vertical laws are coded as printed).
