# Review of `fix/analytic-transient-hot-point`

**Verdict: GO** for the merge into `development`, after the correction below (committed on the branch), with the whole battery left to the tester. After the correction no frozen number moves (`tests/regression` at 2, 1 and 0.5 mm: 317 passed with no pending file of this branch).

## What the branch does

`analytic._local_dynamics` read the transient of a heater with vias or heatsinks at the hottest cell of the board WITHOUT them (`_modal_dynamics(..., 0.0, 0.0)`), while the steady result reports the hottest cell WITH them (`research/transient-reference-boards` README 4.3). The branch reads the argmax of the steady field with them, rebuilds the uniform-mode output row there and calibrates the via couplings at the same point.

## Checks

1. **Code against theory 6 and `analytic_local.py`.** The calibration target (`steady.T` at the point), the uniform row of the modes outside the block (`rest`) and the block row (`ls.output_row`) use the same point as the readout: consistent. Every consumer goes through `_ModalSet`, which takes each heater's point from the system that serves it (`ds[serve[k]]["points"][k]`) and rebuilds the rows of every system there (`_point_vector`): step responses (`step_responses`, used by the profile decision through `api`), the profile runner and its curves (`_AnalyticAdapter.observe`), the animation (`solve_transient`, frames from `field`, which does not depend on the point) and the peak maps (from frames). Several heaters: each is calibrated on a copy with only its power (`_ModalSet`), so the argmax of that copy is its own; with one heater the problem itself is used. Boards without vias and heatsinks keep `_modal_dynamics` (bit-identical, tested).
2. **Re-run.** The author's numbers for the `vias` board (plateau -0.04 %, 0.3 s -7.9 %) reproduce through `tests/test_analytic_hot_point.py`. The 12 boards of validation 4.1 were re-run (Analytic 0.5 mm, 300 terms; the program's FVM at 0.5 mm, and the strong-heatsink board also at 0.25 mm: 11.249 against 11.248 s, converged), in four variants of the readout and the calibration target, plus the FVM read at fixed cells (its own steady hottest node and the cells the Analytic chose) and one square wave per heatsink board.
3. **Frozen numbers.** With the branch as delivered exactly the two declared times to 63 % moved (`example_board|analytic`, `profile_board|analytic`, U1 10.134 to 10.249 s). After the correction none moves and the pending file was removed; `lumped-part-readout.json` is the only pending file on the branch, `fix/drawn-copper-faces` will add an FVM/FDM-only file: no record in two files.

## Findings and corrections

1. **Defect in the program (fixed): the plain argmax picks a noise cell of a flat top.** On the four-layer boards with vias and a heatsink under the part the Analytic steady field is flat to 0.1 % over 18 to 36 cells (the old point 0.03 % and 0.12 % below the maximum); the argmax fell on cells 1 to 2 mm off the centre ((38, 61) and (42, 62) of 80 x 120) that the FVM at 0.5 mm puts 3.0 % and 0.4 % below its hottest node, and moving the part by 0.05 mm moved the argmax by 2 mm and the time to 63 % by 1.3 % (10.05 against 9.92 s). That was the whole of the reported 'worsening' of the heatsink boards: on the 300 W/m2K board the readout moved alone gave 10.10 s, the calibration target moved alone 10.26 s, both 10.05 s, neither 10.31 s (FVM 11.25 s), so it is the readout and not the calibration, the block truncation or the heatsink path. **Rule adopted (the maintainer delegated the choice):** `analytic._readout_point` keeps the point of the board without vias and heatsinks while it is within `READOUT_TOLERANCE` = 0.25 % of the steady maximum, otherwise it reads the hottest cell (nearest to that point among equal maxima). The cases where the hot point really moves leave the old point 0.50 % (vias and a heatsink beside the part) and 0.57 % (the via cluster of the study) below; the noise cases are 0.03 to 0.12 %. Tests that fail on the branch as delivered: `test_a_flat_top_keeps_the_point_and_the_response_is_continuous`, `test_readout_point_rule`, and `test_four_layers_with_a_strong_heatsink` with its limit restored to 10 %. The restriction to 'vias only' was rejected (the noise board has vias); keeping the plain argmax with a -11 % warning was rejected (a warning for an error the fix itself made, and a discontinuous readout).
2. **Explanation corrected: the +2.1 % of vias with a heatsink beside the part is the reference's readout.** The program's FVM step response is the hottest node at every time, which moves during the step (centre of the part early, the side away from the heatsink later). At its fixed steady hottest node, the Analytic's new point, the FVM gives 34.04 s and the Analytic 34.13 s (+0.26 %; at the old point -1.8 % against the FVM there). The fix is right on that board; the program metric reads +2.07 %.
3. **Wrong document text (fixed):** the warning key and texts said '-11 % to +2 %'; with the rule the heatsink sample is -8.3 to +2.1 %, so '-8 % to +2 %' (analytic.py, the user guide twice, `tests/test_analytic_transient_warning.py`, CHANGELOG, models 5, theory 6, versions). `docs/models.md` item 5 had a stray ').,'. The validation section claimed 'the other eight unchanged to 0.01 point' and '-0.48 % before' for the 100 W/m2K board; the re-run gives +0.30 % before (the author's sampling of the step response differs), now in the table.
4. **Section number:** `development` ends at 50 (its sections 49 and 50 are out of order in the file, left as they are) and `fix/drawn-copper-faces` (not merged) writes 51. This branch now takes **52**, so the two can merge in either order without renumbering (if the other branch is dropped, 51 stays empty; renumber then).

## Limits, stated

The 0.25 % is a measured rule on the boards above, not a bound: a board whose true hot point moves by less than 0.25 % of the rise keeps the old point (plateau at most 0.25 % low), and a case between the groups would choose by the threshold. The 4-layer 300 W/m2K board stays -8.3 % (the coupled block, as validation 4.1 says), the early error of the Analytic with vias (-7.9 % at 0.3 s) is untouched, and the warning's other numbers (up to +4 % on further boards, 5 to 7 % low peaks of two overlapping parts over vias) were measured before this change and not re-measured. The finite-element numbers of the `vias` board were read from the research folder, not re-run.
