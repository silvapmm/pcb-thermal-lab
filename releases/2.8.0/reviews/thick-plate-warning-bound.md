# Review of `fix/thick-plate-warning-bound`

**Verdict: GO** for the merge into `development`, after the corrections below (committed on the branch), with the whole battery left to the tester. Text and warning only: no number of the program moves (`tests/regression` at 2 mm passed).

## What the branch does

It corrects the bound of the thick-plate warning (`checks.thick_plate_bound`, `check_thick_plates`): the surface under a source minus the node of the layered models is `(q'' + q_f) t / (2 k)`, the through-thickness gradient plus the half plate between the node and the face film (the layered models put the film on the node), at most `q'' t / k`; the film term enters when `h t / (2 k)` exceeds 1e-3. Documents: theory 1.2 and 7.5 (7.6b), models 4b, validation 22 and 22.1, the user guide, versions and the CHANGELOG.

## Checks

1. **Derivation.** One-dimensional plate, flux `q''` into the top, film `h1` on the top and `h2` on the bottom, one node with no vertical resistance (as the models): the exact surface-minus-node difference is `q'' (t/k) h2^2 / ((h1 + h2)(h1 + h2 + h1 h2 t/k))`, which is `q'' t / k` with no top film and never more. Through the thickness alone the profile is linear and the face is `q'' t / (2 k)` above the column mean (`t / (3 k)` is volumetric generation, as the branch says). For a localised source the film term is, to first order in `r = t / (2 k)`, `r` times a weighted mean of the film flux with weights `h G(hot, x)` that sum to at most 1, so `r h rise` (or `r q''`, by the maximum principle `h theta_max <= q''`) is an upper value; with sideways spreading the vertical flux under the source falls with depth, so the through-thickness term is an upper value too. Both are estimates from one-dimensional conduction, not rigorous bounds for every geometry, and the text now says 'an upper estimate'.
2. **Independent 3D check** (`tests/helpers/layered3d.py`, 16 sub-slabs per plate, 1 mm cells, the program's FVM at 0.5 mm, the same board on both sides: copper conductivity, the thin laminate of a one-layer board, isotropic dielectric, the film under the source). The single plates (thin copper board, 1.6 mm of k 0.3, 10 mm aluminium, 20 mm steel with a 10 and a 40 mm source, a case-2-like plate) held: quoted 0.003 / 17.5 / 10.4 / 84.0 / 20.9 / 8.7 K against 3D surface minus FVM -0.04 / 12.55 / 5.74 / 33.12 / 18.72 / 4.79 K. The case 2 numbers of the study (2.90 / 5.55 / 4.66 / 3.79 K, column 1.77 K against 1.87 K) were read from the research README and agree with the test.
3. **Every consumer.** The warning reaches the Results summary, the report (`WARNING:` line), the command line and the warning box (short title by `warning_title`); the texts name no branch or study (`test_warning_texts_plain`). The typical user: a new project has typed coefficients (`defaults.json` `h_mode` manual), so the film term is applied by default.
4. **Other warnings.** The block and fin warnings and the edge warnings speak of other effects; no double warning. The user guide's 'the 3D solid shows a real effect 2 to 10 times smaller' was true only for blocks fed through the board and contradicted the case 2 column (0.95 of the bound): corrected.

## Findings and corrections

1. **Defect in the program (fixed): the bound was not one on a board of two or more layers.** The vertical links of the layered models start at the copper node, so heat leaving the copper of the part into the laminate also misses a half plate, and every other copper layer it crosses is one node and misses its whole drop. Two 3 mm plates of k 20 over 0.05 mm of k 3, h 400 below: 3D surface minus FVM 17.9 K (20 mm source, 50 W) and 14.9 K (40 mm, 100 W) against 9.4 and 4.7 K quoted (the 40 mm source was silent at 13 % of its rise). Now on such a board `q_f = q''` and the other copper layers add `q'' t_l / k` (the one-dimensional column); quoted 37.5 and 18.8 K. For PCB copper it stays negligible (four 105 um layers, 1 W on 1 mm: estimate 1.1 K, measured 0.10 K, rise 138 K; four 400 um layers, 5 W on 3 mm: 2.3 K against 0.21 K; none fires). Test `test_two_thick_plates_quote_the_face_towards_the_laminate` (fails on dda15938).
2. **Defect in the program (fixed): computed coefficients dropped the film term.** The run has the coefficients of its last Picard step (`extras["h_top"]`, `["h_bottom"]`); `api` passes them (`check_thick_plates(..., h_faces)`). A 10 mm plate of k 20 with computed natural convection now quotes the film term. Test `test_computed_coefficients_enter_the_film_term` (fails on dda15938).
3. **Defect in the program (fixed): the film of a metal block.** `_face_film_h` added `h_top + h_bottom` for a block on a one-layer board and left out the fin ratio; the block now uses `blocks.exposed_h` (the coefficient of `blocks.extra_layers`). Not measured against a 3D reference (no thick poor-conductor block with a strong film was run).
4. **Wording (fixed).** 'The value is a bound' became 'an upper estimate from conduction straight through the plate'; the warning names each term; the short title of the warning box was 'U1: the copper of layer 1' (cut at the parenthesis of the dimensions), now 'U1: the copper of layer 1 is thick and poorly conducting' (test added).
5. **Documents (fixed).** theory 7.5, models 4b, validation 22.1 (the table of the independent check and the plain statement that the first wording of the branch was not a bound on two plates), the user guide, versions (the planned `fix/face-film-half-slab` removes only the film term) and the CHANGELOG.

## Limits, stated

An estimate, not a theorem: a three-dimensional geometry with the heat focused under the source (a poor plate over a much better one, a source near a corner) was not searched for a counter-example beyond the nine plates. The trigger stays at 5 %. The film term of a block and the 3D solid as the reference model were not measured.
