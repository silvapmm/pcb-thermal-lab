# Text proposed for docs/theory.md (surface and mean through the thickness)

**Which temperature is a layer node.** Every layered model (FVM, FDM, Lumped, Analytic) carries one temperature per copper layer, the mean over its thickness. A thermocouple or an infrared camera on the copper reads the SURFACE. For a layer of thickness t and conductivity k whose field varies slowly in the plane, the heat balance of the column gives a parabolic profile and
`T_top - T_m = t (2 q_top - q_bot) / (6 k)`, with `q_top` and `q_bot` the net fluxes entering through the two faces; a column fed on top and cooled below gives `q t / 2k`. The difference is the through-thickness term; it is not the in-plane difference between the mean and the maximum over the source (the spreading resistance of Muzychka and Yovanovich uses the in-plane mean over the source at the surface).

**When it matters.** For a plate with a film h on the cooled face the two groups are Bi = h t / k and t / a (a the source side). Lienhard prints Bi << 1, and about 3 % for Bi <= 0.1, as the condition of a body that is nearly uniform through its thickness. For a centred square source on a plate five source sides wide the closed series gives (surface - mean) / surface rise below 0.1 % for 35 to 105 um copper on FR-4, 3.5 % for 2 mm of copper cooled at 1000 W/m2K under a 5 mm part, 7.5 % for a 1.5 mm aluminium base at 2000 W/m2K, 13 % for the plate of case 2 of Muzychka, Culham and Yovanovich (2003) and 41 % for a 10 mm aluminium cold plate at 5000 W/m2K. A Joule-heated bar with a film on both faces has the mean Bi/6 of the rise above its surface (0.002 % for 3 mm of copper in air).

**What the program reports.** The layer mean (the node). The surface is offered as an estimate by the parabola, accurate to 7 % of the difference up to t/a = 0.5. Sources: [Muzychka2003], [Lienhard2024], [TI-SPRA953B], [ROHM-64AN113E], [Razavi2016Thesis]. Figures: where_read, decision, biot_map.
