# Text proposed for docs/theory.md 1.2 (surface and mean through the thickness)

**Which temperature is a layer node.** Every layered model (FVM, FDM, Lumped, Analytic) and the 3D solid (one copper slab per layer) carries one temperature per copper layer, the mean over its thickness (the column). A thermocouple or an infrared camera on the copper reads the SURFACE, and so do the board and case temperatures of the datasheet metrics (psi_JT on the top centre of the package, psi_JB on the centre pin, [ROHM-64AN113E], [TI-SPRA953B]; read on the page image). For a layer of thickness t and conductivity k whose in-plane Laplacian is the same at every depth, the heat balance of the column gives a parabolic profile and
`T_top - T_m = t (2 q_top - q_bot) / (6 k)`, with `q_top` and `q_bot` the net fluxes entering through the two faces; a column fed on top and cooled below gives `q t / 2k`. This through-thickness term is not the in-plane difference between the mean and the maximum over a source: the spreading resistance of Muzychka, Culham and Yovanovich uses the in-plane mean over the source of the SURFACE temperature ([Muzychka2003], eq. 3 and 4).

**When it matters.** For a plate cooled by a film h on its other face three groups decide: Bi = h t / k, t / a (a the source side) and the plate width W / a. Lienhard gives Bi << 1 (about 3 % for Bi <= 0.1) as the condition of a body that is nearly uniform ([Lienhard2024] p. 23 to 24 and 68); under a source narrower than the plate that is necessary but not sufficient. A closed cosine series (checked against the independent 3D solver) gives (surface - mean) / surface rise at the source centre, for a plate five source sides wide: below 0.1 % for 35 to 105 um copper on FR-4, 3.5 % for 2 mm of copper cooled at 1000 W/m2K under a 5 mm part, 7.5 % for a 1.5 mm aluminium base at 2000 W/m2K, 13 % for the plate of case 2 of [Muzychka2003] with one centred 10 mm source and 41 % for a 10 mm aluminium cold plate at 5000 W/m2K; a wider plate reads more (the 2 mm copper plate 50 mm wide under a 4 mm source: 10.7 %; a 10 mm plate of k 50: 54 %), because the film rise of the whole plate is smaller. A Joule-heated bar with a film on both faces has its mean Bi/6 of the rise above its surface (0.002 % for 3 mm of copper in air).

**What the program reports.** The layer mean, in every table, in Tj, the margins, the top path and every comparison. The FVM and FDM add the surface of a thick outer copper layer (above 105 um) as an estimate by the parabola, within 7 % of the through-thickness difference up to t/a = 0.5 and an upper estimate beyond; it is a view (Layer box 'Surface (top)', 'Surface (bottom)'), never a mode.
