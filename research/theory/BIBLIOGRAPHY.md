# Bibliography of the theory

One list for every topic of this folder. Each entry has a **key** (used in the `Sources` of the topic files, for example `[Patankar1980]`), the citation as the repository records it, the address as recorded, a **reading status** and the topics that use it.

**Reading status** (the rule of `CLAUDE.md`, "Method and honesty": never present a guess as a finding):

| Code | Meaning |
| :-- | :-- |
| **R** | **recorded as read** by the project document that cites it (`docs/research.md`, `docs/validation.md`, the READMEs of the investigations); the repository cannot show how much of each page was seen, and the session notes in `CLAUDE.md` say that WebFetch reached few sites, so some "read" pages were seen through a search summary; where a topic leans on one number, the topic says so |
| **S** | seen only as a search summary, a snippet or a converted text with missing figures; not a citation to lean on |
| **M** | named by the project documents (`docs/theory.md` section 12 and others) or known from memory; **not opened** in this work; the journal details marked "memory" were not checked |
| **N** | named and wanted, but could not be opened (host blocked, login wall) or not yet obtained |
| **L** | local: an own computation in the repository (a study script), not a publication |

This file records what the repository already says, **plus two rounds of checks made later** (the second is the maintainer's reference compilation, kept as received in `docs/reference-compilation.md`, which read several pages in full and corrected earlier citations: the Hashin-Shtrikman conductivity paper is J. Appl. Phys. 33 (1962), not the elastic paper of 1963; "Lush, May 2004" is not identifiable; the Gerber revision is 2024.05; and the Python `ht` source, read here, shows that the Hollands example in its documentation takes the Grashof number and that its code, not its docstring, is the form that works). The first round from the session: the web search tool worked (it returns summaries, so what it confirms has status S), the page-fetch tool and direct downloads were blocked for every host tried (Wikipedia, Ucamco, Mouser, the ReadTheDocs pages), and only the Python package index could be read (status R for package metadata). That round confirmed the citations of Churchill and Chu (1975), Churchill (1977), Culham and Yovanovich (1998), Patankar (1980), Milton and Torquato (2002), Briggs and others (2000), the Nexperia note on Foster and Cauer, the version and licence of pyamg; it could not find Lee et al. (1995) at all. The properties of the 2D effective-medium formulas were checked numerically in `scripts/check_mapping_formulas.py`. The list of what should still be read to close the gaps is at the end (section 14).

## 1. Numerical methods: finite volumes, differences, time, solvers, verification

### [Patankar1980]

Patankar, *Numerical Heat Transfer and Fluid Flow*, Hemisphere (McGraw-Hill), 1980, xiii + 197 pages, ISBN 0-89116-522-3 (CRC reissue 978-0-89116-522-4), bibliography pp. 189-193 (library catalogue records and The maintainer's reference compilation (`docs/reference-compilation.md`); the book itself was not opened). ### [McCormickTamstorf]
S. McCormick and R. Tamstorf (byline to confirm on the title page), a multigrid primer, https://grandmaster.colorado.edu/~stevem/mgprimer.pdf . **Read in The maintainer's reference compilation (`docs/reference-compilation.md`).** Contents: 1 introduction, 2 preliminaries, 3 smoothing, 4 coarse-grid correction, 5 a word about residuals, 6 multilevel solvers (V- and W-cycles), 7 full multigrid, 8 basic theory, 9 development, 10 summary, appendix A approximation properties. Formulas extracted there: Richardson smoothing `v <- v - (1/||L||)(L v - g)`, error propagation `G = I - L/||L||`; restriction `R = alpha P^t`; Galerkin coarse operator `L^(2h) = R L^h P`; coarse-grid correction `v <- v - P (L^(2h))^(-1) R (L v - g)`; the V-cycle as relax mu times, restrict the residual, solve the coarse problem recursively from a zero guess, correct, relax nu times; the W-cycle visits the coarse problem twice; two-grid convergence `||TG||^2 <= gamma < 1`; Theorem 1: with the strong approximation property a V(0,1)-cycle has `||MV||^2 <= 1 - 1/C`; full multigrid reduces the error by about `2^(-(m + 1/2))` per level and, for the model Poisson problem, a V(2,1)-cycle is typically enough; the cost of a cycle is proportional to the unknowns of the finest level. **Status R (compilation).** Topic 16. **Byline and title confirmed** from the arXiv page (arXiv:2605.17828, version 1 of 18 May 2026): *Multigrid Primer: Basic Principles*, Stephen F. McCormick and Rasmus Tamstorf; the abstract read there says it presents multigrid as a V-cycle iterative solver in a variational view (symmetric positive definite systems from elliptic PDEs) and then full multigrid. The Colorado address above returned a 404 when fetched, so the PDF may have moved; the contents list and formulas above stay as the compilation gave them.

### [EC1998Spreading]
Seri Lee, *Calculating Spreading Resistance in Heat Sinks*, Electronics Cooling, 1 January 1998, https://www.electronics-cooling.com/1998/01/calculating-spreading-resistance-in-heat-sinks/ . **Opened** through the page converter: the article is by Seri Lee alone, and the formulas for the centre-mounted source (equations 1 and 2) and the correction factor of Figure 4 are images that did not come through (**N for those**). What the text gives: for a source at a corner or an edge, `Rc = C x Rc(A_p, A_s, k, t/C, R0/C)` with `C = 2` for the corner; total `R_total = R0 + Cf x Rc`; accurate to about 5 % for source and base shapes with aspect ratio up to 2.5; `Cf = 1` is acceptable for a source nearer the centre than the edges (5 to 10 % error); for a planar-fin sink the base thickness is increased by 20 % to account for the spreading in the fins, for a pin-fin sink nothing; Kennedy's solution is the limit `R0 -> 0` and can grossly underestimate when `R0` is not small. **Worked example** (aluminium base 100 x 100 x 1.3 mm, source 25 x 25 mm, `k = 200 W/mK`, `R0 = 1.0 K/W`): centre `Rc = 0.66` (total 1.66 K/W), edge midpoint 1.29 (2.29), corner 2.38 (3.38). The centre case is the one `block_contact_study.py` reproduces (0.66 and 1.66); **the edge and corner cases are not yet compared** with the program's plate model and are the next independent check. Whether the article presents the model of `[Lee1995]` was not seen in the converted text. **Status S (numbers R, formulas N).** Topic 18.

### [ElenbaasBCR]
W. Elenbaas, Physica 9 (1942), *Heat dissipation of parallel plates by free convection* (pages "1-28" from memory; the original was not opened), and A. Bar-Cohen and W. M. Rohsenow, *Thermally Optimum Spacing of Vertical, Natural Convection Cooled, Parallel Plates*, J. Heat Transfer 106 (1) (1984) 116-123 (abstract seen: they maximise the heat transfer per unit volume or per unit primary area for symmetric or asymmetric, isothermal or isoflux plates and combine the fully developed channel and single-plate asymptotes by composite relations; also as described at https://abcm.org.br/anais/cobem/2003/html/pdf/COB03-1466.pdf, where the constants could not be read). From The maintainer's reference compilation (`docs/reference-compilation.md`) and the Maya channel page and a course note (all secondary): **Elenbaas** `Nu = (Ra*/24)[1 - exp(-35/Ra*)]^(3/4)`, `Ra* = Ra_S (S/L) = g beta dT S^4/(nu alpha L)`, for 0.1 < Ra* < 1e5 (isothermal, symmetric, air), with the limits `Ra*/24` and, at large Ra*, about `0.62 Ra*^(1/4)`; optimum `Ra* about 50` (Simons). **Bar-Cohen and Rohsenow**, isothermal symmetric plates: `Nu_S = [576/(Ra_S S/L)^2 + 2.873/(Ra_S S/L)^(1/2)]^(-1/2)` (Incropera Eq. 9.45), a Churchill-Usagi blend with n = -2 whose limits are `Ra*/24` and `0.590 Ra*^(1/4)`; optimum spacing `S_opt about 2.714 L / Ra_L^(1/4)` with `Nu_opt about 1.307` (from memory, marked "verify"). Checked here (`scripts/check_correlations.py`): both limits of the composite are reproduced; `2.714^4 = 54.3`, close to Simons' 50; the Elenbaas form tends to `Ra*/24` and, at large Ra*, to within about 3 % of `0.62 Ra*^(1/4)`. Aung et al. channel correlations (Maya page): `m = -1.9, c = 1.2, C_l = 0.515` (isothermal, air, to Ra about 1e5) and `m = -3.5, c = 1.07, H_l = 0.624` (uniform flux). Related: Bodoia and Osterle, J. Heat Transfer 84 (1962); Sparrow and Bahrami, J. Heat Transfer 102 (1980); Bejan (1984, 1995); Fujii and Miyatake (1972). **Status S for the citations and the forms, M for the optimum.** The program's fins do not use these (a uniform coefficient). Topics 11, 13.

## 3. Convection and radiation

### [ChurchillChu1975]
S. W. Churchill and H. H. S. Chu, *Correlating Equations for Laminar and Turbulent Free Convection from a Vertical Plate*, Int. J. Heat Mass Transfer 18 (11) (1975) 1323-1329, doi 10.1016/0017-9310(75)90243-4. The paper itself was not opened. The correlation used by `convection.py`, `Nu = {0.825 + 0.387 Ra^(1/6) / [1 + (0.492/Pr)^(9/16)]^(8/27)}^2` (all Rayleigh numbers), was confirmed by three independent secondary sources (The maintainer's reference compilation (`docs/reference-compilation.md`): the Python `ht` library, pychemqt, Incropera tables), and **the source of the `ht` package (version 1.2.0, MIT licence) was read in this work: its worked example, `Nu(Pr 0.69, Gr 2.63e9) = 147.16`, from Bergman and others, is reproduced to the digit by the program's form** (`scripts/check_correlations.py`). The laminar form, slightly more accurate for Ra up to about 1e9, is `Nu = 0.68 + 0.670 Ra^(1/4) / [1 + (0.492/Pr)^(9/16)]^(4/9)`; against the all-range form it is +6.6 % at Ra 1e5, -5.2 % at 1e7 and -25 % at 1e9 for Pr 0.71 (the all-range form includes the turbulent rise). A vertical cylinder can be treated as a plate if `D/L >= 35 / Gr_L^(1/4)`; inclined plates use `g cos(theta)` up to 60 degrees. Companion paper on the horizontal cylinder: IJHMT 18 (9) (1975) 1049, `Nu_D = {0.60 + 0.387 Ra_D^(1/6) / [1 + (0.559/Pr)^(9/16)]^(8/27)}^2` for `Ra_D <= 1e12`. **Status S for the paper (correlation confirmed), R for the implementation of the form in `ht`.** Topic 08. **The two forms seen in secondary sources (the paper was never opened):** the laminar form `Nu = 0.68 + 0.670 Ra^(1/4) / [1 + (0.492/Pr)^(9/16)]^(4/9)` for Ra below 1e9 and the all-range form (the page of `thermal.mayahtt.com`, which cites Incropera and DeWitt, 3rd edition, equations 9.26 and 9.27 on p. 543; the library `ht`, whose worked example `Nu(Pr 0.69, Gr 2.63e9) = 147.16` is Example 9.2 of Bergman and others; and [Yovanovich1993Flat], who writes the laminar blend of Churchill without the 0.68).

### [ChurchillBlend]
S. W. Churchill, *A comprehensive correlating equation for laminar, assisting, forced and free convection*, AIChE Journal 23 (1) (1977) 10-16 (confirmed by citation lists, The maintainer's reference compilation (`docs/reference-compilation.md`)). The paper was not opened. The mixed-convection blending `Nu^n = Nu_forced^n +- Nu_free^n` (plus for assisting, minus for opposing flow), with n = 3 as the usual value for external flows, is written there **from memory and marked "verify"**: whether the program's cube-root rule `h = (h_n^3 + h_f^3)^(1/3)` is this paper's rule is not confirmed (the program uses the plus sign only). Related: the general rule of Churchill and Usagi (below). Other Churchill papers confirmed: Churchill and Churchill, AIChE J. 21 (3) (1975) 604; Churchill, Ind. Eng. Chem. Fundam. 16 (1) (1977) 109-116; Churchill and Bernstein, J. Heat Transfer 99 (2) (1977) 300-306; Churchill, Lett. Heat Mass Transfer 4 (3) (1977) 193-199. **Status S for the citation, M for the rule.** Topic 08.

### [ChurchillUsagi]
S. W. Churchill and R. Usagi, a general expression for the correlation of rates of transfer and other phenomena, AIChE J. 18 (1972) 1121-1128 (volume and pages from memory); extension in Ind. Eng. Chem. Fundam. 13 (1) (1974) 39-44, doi 10.1021/i160049a008 (The maintainer's reference compilation (`docs/reference-compilation.md`); abstracts seen only). The rule: `Y = (1 + Z^n)^(1/n)`, with Y and Z built from the asymptotic solutions for large and small values of the independent variable; n comes from plots of Y against Z and Y/Z against 1/Z and is insensitive, so the nearest integer is acceptable; the power-mean form is `Phi^n = Phi_0^n + Phi_inf^n`. The composite of Bar-Cohen and Rohsenow is this form with n = -2. **Status S.** Topics 08, 13.

### [Hollands]

### [KellerDykhne]
J. B. Keller, J. Math. Phys. 5 (1964) 548, and A. M. Dykhne, Zh. Eksp. Teor. Fiz. 59 (1970) 110 (English translation in Sov. Phys. JETP; the maintainer's compilation writes "Sov. Phys. JETP 59 (1970) 110", which mixes the Russian volume with the English journal name, and an earlier note here gave "JETP 7, 110" from a course solution, which is probably a wrong volume; the translation volume is not confirmed).
