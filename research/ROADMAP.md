### A. Models and numerics
* **A1. Subdivide the gap of every multilayer board** (#9). Does more than one node through the gap remove the error against a resolved 3D solution? Why: a 2-layer board reads 2 to 8 % low in the FVM (-2.3, -3.8, -7.7 % on three boards; `power-calculator` 4.5 to 4.7; the one-layer case is fixed, `effective-conductivity` 4.11); the measurement ran from a scratch directory and is not versioned. Open. Next step: the sweep of the one-layer laminate on boards with full copper, a pad, a part and vias; then a change of the 2- and 3-layer results.
* **A2. Why the Analytic model is hot on one-layer boards with partial copper** (#10). Why: -32 % to -13 % on a 2-layer board with a 20 mm pad (`power-calculator` 4.7). Open. Next step: a pad of growing size against the independent solver to separate the causes.
* **A3. The 3D solid with a rotated square at a high side coefficient** (#11). Why: +1.15 K above the aligned square (reviews of `solid3d-verification` and `outline`). Open. Next step: rotate square and board edge separately on a copy, vary `h_side` and the cell.
* **A5. The point via and the cell** (#7). Which treatment makes the FVM/FDM via converge: the Peaceman well index or always spreading the barrel over its disc? Why: example board -4.49 / -3.89 / -3.59 K at 1 / 0.5 / 0.25 mm; error 0.1 to 0.3 K at 0.5 to 1 mm (`via-fill/INTEGRATION.md` 1). Studied in `point-via-convergence/` (reviewed): neither plan is enough; a mean-potential coupling with a centred footprint and a Peaceman well (target radius 0.78 r_v) converges within 0.7 % of the via effect from 1 to 0.0625 mm against the layered reference (the layered via with 0.78 r_v is itself -2 to +4 % from a 3D barrel, review); the 3D solid varies because its rule changes with the via area (`point-via-convergence/INTEGRATION.md`). Next step: the maintainer's decision on `fix/point-via` (docs/versions.md 'Planned').
* **A6. Why Lumped and Analytic overstate vias** (#8). Why: copper-fill gain 35 to 52 % too large, -6.3 to -7.0 K against -3.9 to -4.5 K (`via-fill` 4.5 and 4.6). **Studied and reviewed** (`lumped-analytic-vias`; the review found the Lumped cluster term worse than the program on dense arrays of the example board, 18 to 32 %): the cause is the missing constriction resistance `(ln(R/r_t) - 3/4)/(2 pi S)` of every plane at the barrel (the program's Lumped model equals the closed form without it to the digit on a lattice cell); fixes measured as copies in memory: Lumped with a Krogh well per plane and a cluster term (copper-fill gain of the example board 6.79 to 4.47 K against 4.20 K), Analytic with a tile self-term, a bilinear spread and 1 mm tiles (4.27 K; mean error 0.3 to 2.3 % on simple boards); the old model as a legacy option with a warning. Left open: the Lumped lone via and row outside the footprint (ring-dependent, 9 to 31 % of a small effect), dense arrays of open barrels in the Lumped model (8 to 24 % under), Gerber boards with many vias, a published via-array constriction case reproduced (none openly readable found).
* **A7. The residual between Analytic and FVM with insulated edges**. Why: the 0.3 to 1.0 % is the edge loss the series leaves out; with insulated edges -0.04 to -0.29 % is unexplained (`theory` 7). Open (small). Next step: series truncation and cell-centre against the mode projection.
* **A8. A time simulation with a block, and of the 3D solid**. Why: steady state only today (`block-and-fins/INTEGRATION.md`, `solid3d-verification` 8). Open. Next step: FVM transient with a block as the reference; a lumped capacity in the ring network.
* **A9. The outline: scaling, holes and parts inside a cut**. Why: the same-area rectangle is 10 to 18 % cool near the edge or in a T arm (`outline` 4.2); the centroid scaling and the lost edge over many outlines are printed by no script. Open. Next step: scripts for both; maps of the stretched field against the FVM. **Asked by the maintainer: Lumped and Analytic on a board that is not a rectangle** run today on the rectangle of the same area with a warning in the Results summary (`docs/validation.md` 13: 0.4 to 2.9 % away from the edge, Lumped 6 to 18 % and Analytic 0 to 16 % cool near the edge or in the arm of a T); no published method was found. Paths to study: correct the scaling near the edge, warn earlier when a part is close to the edge, or keep the approximation and recommend the FVM (which solves any outline). A time simulation of these two models on an outline does not exist either.
* **A10. Multigrid and the solver timings**. Why: timings quoted by the program documents are printed by no script (`theory` 7); topic 16 has no source for its formulas. Open. Next step: time direct, CG, SOR and AMG-CG on the example and a 1 million unknown board.
* **A12. A spot without copper under a source, in the layered models.** Does one node per copper layer, with the laminate averaged through its thickness, underestimate the temperature where the hottest cell under a part is bare laminate? Why: on a real board drawn as polygons the FVM lost another 12.5 to 14.4 points of the peak against the independent solver on the program's own cell fill, only where the hottest cell had no copper (fill 0.00); where it was copper the loss was under 0.4 points; the bound `q'' t / (2 k)` of `docs/theory.md` is about 35 K for 1 W over 36 mm2 and 0.77 mm of laminate (`zone-union` README 8.3). It is a limit of the layered idealisation (FVM, FDM, Lumped, Analytic), not of the zone rule; the 3D solid resolves it. Also, the reference of that study is not converged at 0.25 mm (clearances of about 0.2 mm are bridged by partly covered cells; 0.125 mm raises the peak by 4.7 to 13.5 %), so every error quoted there is a lower bound. **Studied and reviewed** (`bare-spot-under-source`): against a resolved 3D reference (0.3 to 2.2 % above the converged value, README 9) the FVM peak over a 6 mm bare window is -18.4 % (two layers 1.6 mm), -6.6 % (0.8 mm), -3.8 % (4-layer typical) and -3.7 % (6-layer); the 3D solid is within 2 % (0.4 % at the face: it reports the centre of its top copper slab); two causes separated: the vertical path of a bare cell (a free correction removes the whole error of the 4- and 6-layer boards) and, on thick gaps, the lateral conductance of the half-gap node (laminate nodes next to the bare cells with a margin of one gap thickness: -18.4 % -> -3.1 % with 4); the Analytic does not see windows (-11 to -63 % for windows up to 12 mm) and the Lumped is erratic (A2, A6). Proposals P1 to P3 in its `INTEGRATION.md`. **Still open:** the FDM with the construction, the transient capacity of the extra nodes, windows narrower than the gap (-9 % left), a real board other than the Stormduino, a published case of a part over a bare window (none found).
* **A13. The SOR solver with the mean-potential via couplings** (#11c; linked to A5). Why: the SOR stops 19 to 27 K cool once the vias are mean-potential couplings, because `solve_sor` iterates the stencil, not the matrix. Studied in `sor-with-via-couplings/`: cause isolated on a toy with a closed form and simple boards, block SOR reaches the direct solution to 1e-8 K in the same sweeps as the board without vias; reviewed (README 8): the block step must be relaxed and the blocks coloured (c'), convergence proved; open: the maintainer's choice, and Young's omega of the board without vias as a separate fix (1.9613 against 1.8609 on the 3 x 3 board, 6.0 times fewer sweeps), which needs a cheap estimate of the Jacobi spectral radius.
* **A11. Laminate nodes for patterned copper** (#12). Why: two nodes are 5.5 to 13.6 % cool on seven real windows, 1.9 and 5.3 % with eight (`gerber-models` R2). Open. Next step: sweep 1 to 8 nodes on the windows, the nine boards and the pads; a change of `laminate.py`. Link with A12: `bare-spot-under-source` measured n = 1 to 6 laminate nodes in the gap of the **cells that show laminate** (plus a margin of one gap thickness) on two-layer and typical 4- and 6-layer boards, with a vertical correction for the copper-free thickness; the same mask (nodes only where the copper is partly absent, the single link elsewhere) is the candidate for the one-layer laminate nodes of `laminate.py`, which use two nodes everywhere; not measured on a one-layer board there.

## 3. The long runs: done

The twelve long runs were made on a quiet machine and compared with the READMEs; the results and the machine are in the last section of each README and in `gerber-models/README.md` R7.
