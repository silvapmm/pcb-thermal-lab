## 2. The pool of questions

Format: question; Why (evidence, with its pointer); status; Next step (the first study, then what would change in the program).

### A. Models and numerics
* **A1. Subdivide the gap of every multilayer board** (#9). Does more than one node through the gap remove the error against a resolved 3D solution? **Studied and reviewed in `gap-subdivision/`**: the shortfall belongs to bare laminate around a pad on a thick gap, not to every 2-layer board (uniform boards: FVM within -0.72 to +1.04 %, FDM -1.31 to +0.19 % of an exact series); extra nodes in the cells that show laminate remove the through-thickness error like 1 / n; the baseline of the program on simple boards is in `gap-subdivision/baseline/`. Left open from it: the FDM, the transient, vias, blocks and outlines with extra nodes; the other Gerber boards; A2 for the Analytic. (The 3D solid's +0.4 to +5.1 % on small pads is explained: its in-plane error and its four slabs per gap, `gap-subdivision` README 10.2.)
* **A2. Why the Analytic model is too cool (not hot) on boards with partial copper** (#10). Why: -32 % to -13 % on a 2-layer board with a 20 mm pad (the source is `docs/validation.md` section 21, Analytic variants on the CSD17308Q3 board, made with a script that is not in the repository: not reproduced; earlier rows pointed to `power-calculator` 4.7, where the numbers are not; a negative error means the Analytic is too **cool**). **Studied and reviewed** (`analytic-partial-copper`): against a resolved 3D reference the Analytic is -37 to +9 % on a one-layer board with a pad of 6 to 40 mm and -65 to -0.6 % on two layers; the series itself is not the cause (no pad edge: 8e-6 from the number of modes), the cost splits into the **uniform sheet** (the K0-weighted fill; the same physical board entered with one layer or with two layers and a bare second one differs by up to 1.8 times) and the **nodes** (one per copper layer; the laminate of a one-layer board as a film); a candidate (copper as a lateral defect on a bare base through the program's own tile Green-function machinery, plus laminate nodes) is within 3 % on 46 of the 52 pad rows of the `gap-subdivision` baseline where it runs, uniform boards unchanged; limits: tile of at most a sixth of the part and copper edges on tile edges (an edge inside a tile: -3 to -8 %), a part as small as its pad, a window in full copper (-6 %), heaters on the first layer only, vias and blocks not combined, a dense tile system of 2 600 to 10 000 unknowns on a 100 x 80 mm board with pours. The review found the program's warning silent on the two worst baseline rows (-66.7 and -62.0 %, thin gaps) and measured a second trigger; it advises a new trigger and text (P1) and the documented limit (P3), not the candidate (P2).
* **A3. The 3D solid with a rotated square at a high side coefficient** (#11). Why: +1.15 K above the aligned square (reviews of `solid3d-verification` and `outline`). **Studied and reviewed in `rotated-square-3d/`**: the effect is in the program today (+0.8 to +1.8 K at `h_side` 100, +2.5 to +3.1 K at 1000, within 0.3 K at the default 10, 1 mm cells, 40 mm board), it falls only slowly with the cell (+2.0 to +2.3 K at 160 cells per side), a straight edge shifted by half a cell shows it too, so the cause is the treatment of the cells that the outline cuts (conductivity scaled by the cover, fixed half-cell distance to the edge); a round board against an independent axisymmetric reference gives +6.6 % in the program and +0.5 % in a cut-cell copy at 1 mm. The review confirmed the copy against the continuum value of an independent solver on the shifted and turned squares, corrected the 45 degree and T explanations, found a separate defect (a part crossing the outline gets the power of whole cut cells: +2.2 K in the FVM on the example board cut to a T at the default coefficient) and recommends the documented limit (P2) for A3. Next: the maintainer's decisions; open: patterned copper against a reference, the 45 degree board-turn residual of the copy. **The cut cells of the layered FVM** (README 10, 11, reviewed in 11.8): the questions left are which scheme (`links`, or the face aperture with the boundary film of the literature, which is better on the closed form of a sheet with a rim film), the fill of a cut cell with patterned copper (every scheme spreads 3 to 9 K with the copper held inside the outline), the edges along a lattice direction of the 8 x 8 cover (+0.64 % of the area at 45 degrees, 1 mm) and the cost of exact clipping on Gerber outlines.
* **A5. The point via and the cell** (#7). Which treatment makes the FVM/FDM via converge: the Peaceman well index or always spreading the barrel over its disc? Why: example board -4.49 / -3.89 / -3.59 K at 1 / 0.5 / 0.25 mm; error 0.1 to 0.3 K at 0.5 to 1 mm (`via-fill/INTEGRATION.md` 1). Studied in `point-via-convergence/` (reviewed): neither plan is enough; a mean-potential coupling with a centred footprint and a Peaceman well (target radius 0.78 r_v) converges within 0.7 % of the via effect from 1 to 0.0625 mm against the layered reference (the layered via with 0.78 r_v is itself -2 to +4 % from a 3D barrel, review); the 3D solid varies because its rule changes with the via area (`point-via-convergence/INTEGRATION.md`). Next step: the maintainer's decision on `fix/point-via` (docs/versions.md 'Planned').
* **A6. Why Lumped and Analytic overstate vias** (#8). Why: copper-fill gain 35 to 52 % too large, -6.3 to -7.0 K against -3.9 to -4.5 K (`via-fill` 4.5 and 4.6). **Studied and reviewed** (`lumped-analytic-vias`; the review found the Lumped cluster term worse than the program on dense arrays of the example board, 18 to 32 %): the cause is the missing constriction resistance `(ln(R/r_t) - 3/4)/(2 pi S)` of every plane at the barrel (the program's Lumped model equals the closed form without it to the digit on a lattice cell); fixes measured as copies in memory: Lumped with a Krogh well per plane and a cluster term (copper-fill gain of the example board 6.79 to 4.47 K against 4.20 K), Analytic with a tile self-term, a bilinear spread and 1 mm tiles (4.27 K; mean error 0.3 to 2.3 % on simple boards); the old model as a legacy option with a warning. Left open: the Lumped lone via and row outside the footprint (ring-dependent, 9 to 31 % of a small effect), dense arrays of open barrels in the Lumped model (8 to 24 % under), Gerber boards with many vias, a published via-array constriction case reproduced (none openly readable found). **The Lumped cluster term was taken up in A6b** (`lumped-via-groups`: the double counting inside the footprint rejected, the series placement is the fault, a two-zone footprint replaces it; a published unit cell of Fish 2017 reproduced to 2 %); reviewed (the peak of a core hotter than the rest corrected, so the sparse arrays are within 7 %; the program's 1 mm Lumped raster costs the zone model 3.6 to 8.0 % mean); still open there: the lone via and the rows, elongated footprints and clusters off the centre (flagged by a warning), the raster, a Gerber board, a 3D reference with drawn barrels, the transient of the zoned network.
* **A7. The residual between Analytic and FVM with insulated edges**. Why: the 0.3 to 1.0 % is the edge loss the series leaves out; with insulated edges -0.04 to -0.29 % is unexplained (`theory` 7). Open (small). Next step: series truncation and cell-centre against the mode projection.
* **A8. A time simulation with a block, and of the 3D solid**. Why: steady state only today (`block-and-fins/INTEGRATION.md`, `solid3d-verification` 8). Open. Next step: FVM transient with a block as the reference; a lumped capacity in the ring network.

Next step: scripts for both; maps of the stretched field against the FVM. **Asked by the maintainer: Lumped and Analytic on a board that is not a rectangle** run today on the rectangle of the same area with a warning in the Results summary (`docs/validation.md` 13: 0.4 to 2.9 % away from the edge, Lumped 6 to 18 % and Analytic 0 to 16 % cool near the edge or in the arm of a T); no published method was found. * **A12. A spot without copper under a source, in the layered models.** Does one node per copper layer, with the laminate averaged through its thickness, underestimate the temperature where the hottest cell under a part is bare laminate? Why: on a real board drawn as polygons the FVM lost another 12.5 to 14.4 points of the peak against the independent solver on the program's own cell fill, only where the hottest cell had no copper (fill 0.00); where it was copper the loss was under 0.4 points; the bound `q'' t / (2 k)` of `docs/theory.md` is about 35 K for 1 W over 36 mm2 and 0.77 mm of laminate (`zone-union` README 8.3). It is a limit of the layered idealisation (FVM, FDM, Lumped, Analytic), not of the zone rule; the 3D solid resolves it. Also, the reference of that study is not converged at 0.25 mm (clearances of about 0.2 mm are bridged by partly covered cells; 0.125 mm raises the peak by 4.7 to 13.5 %), so every error quoted there is a lower bound. **Studied and reviewed** (`bare-spot-under-source`): against a resolved 3D reference (0.3 to 2.2 % above the converged value, README 9) the FVM peak over a 6 mm bare window is -18.4 % (two layers 1.6 mm), -6.6 % (0.8 mm), -3.8 % (4-layer typical) and -3.7 % (6-layer); the 3D solid is within 2 % (0.4 % at the face: it reports the centre of its top copper slab); two causes separated: the vertical path of a bare cell (a free correction removes the whole error of the 4- and 6-layer boards) and, on thick gaps, the lateral conductance of the half-gap node (laminate nodes next to the bare cells with a margin of one gap thickness: -18.4 % -> -3.1 % with 4); the Analytic does not see windows (-11 to -63 % for windows up to 12 mm) and the Lumped is erratic (A2, A6). Proposals P1 to P3 in its `INTEGRATION.md`. **Still open:** the FDM with the construction, the transient capacity of the extra nodes, windows narrower than the gap (-9 % left), a real board other than the Stormduino, a published case of a part over a bare window (none found).
* **A13. The SOR solver with the mean-potential via couplings** (#11c; linked to A5). Why: the SOR stops 19 to 27 K cool once the vias are mean-potential couplings, because `solve_sor` iterates the stencil, not the matrix. Studied in `sor-with-via-couplings/`: cause isolated on a toy with a closed form and simple boards, block SOR reaches the direct solution to 1e-8 K in the same sweeps as the board without vias; reviewed (README 8): the block step must be relaxed and the blocks coloured (c'), convergence proved; open: the maintainer's choice, and Young's omega of the board without vias as a separate fix (1.9613 against 1.8609 on the 3 x 3 board, 6.0 times fewer sweeps), which needs a cheap estimate of the Jacobi spectral radius.
* **A11. Laminate nodes for patterned copper** (#12). Why: two nodes are 5.5 to 13.6 % cool on seven real windows, 1.9 and 5.3 % with eight (`gerber-models` R2). Open. Next step: sweep 1 to 8 nodes on the windows, the nine boards and the pads; a change of `laminate.py`.
