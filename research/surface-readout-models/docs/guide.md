# Text for the user guide (`pcbthermal/help/index.html`), the Layer entry and the `n/a` tooltip

* **Surface and column.** The column mean is what the model solves; the surface is the temperature of the face of the outer copper, which is a little hotter under a part when the copper is thick. The FVM, the FDM, the FEM, the Analytic (an estimate) and the 3D solid (computed when the copper has slabs, an estimate with one slab) give both. A surface marked 'estimate' is a value derived from the heat fluxes through the face; marked 'upper estimate' where the part is small against the thickness of the plate (side below twice the thickness).
* **The Lumped model** has no heat flux through the face, so it gives no surface temperature. For a part on an outer face of a thick layer, the guide and the tooltip give the most it can add to the column value: `q'' t / 2k`, in kelvin, a bound on that difference only (it does not include the error of the model itself). On 35 um copper it is a few milli-kelvin and can be ignored.
* **In a time run** the maps, the curves and the animation show the column mean. The surface of a frame (FVM, FDM and Analytic, if the maintainer adopts it) is right when the heat has crossed the layer (a time of 0.3 `t^2/alpha` after the power changes: 1 s for 10 mm of copper, 10 microseconds for 35 um) and is an upper value before.
