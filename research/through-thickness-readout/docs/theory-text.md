**The through-thickness term.** The layered models keep one temperature per copper layer, the mean over the thickness (the node). The face films pass the half layer `t/(2k)` (`Board.face_film_h`), so the node is the column mean. The surface under a source is hotter. For a layer of thickness `t` and through-thickness conductivity `k` whose field varies slowly in the plane, the heat balance of the column gives `k t laplace(T_m) = -(q_top + q_bot)` with `q_top`, `q_bot` the net fluxes entering through the two faces; with `T_z(0) = -q_top / k` and `T_z(t) = q_bot / k` the profile is a parabola and

`T_top - T_m = t (2 q_top - q_bot) / (6 k)`, `T_bottom - T_m = t (2 q_bot - q_top) / (6 k)`.

A column fed on the top and cooled below (`q_bot = -q_top`) gives `q t / (2 k)`, the bound of the thick-plate warning; heat that spreads inside the layer (`q_bot -> 0`) gives `q t / (3 k)`. Against an independent 3D solver (54 plates, thickness 0.035 to 20 mm, source side 4 to 20 mm, k 50 to 385) the parabola is within 7 % of the true difference for `t/a <= 0.5` (a the source side) and overshoots beyond (1.24 to 1.42 at `t/a` 1 to 1.25, 3.1 at 5); the true difference is 0.22 to 0.99 of `q t / (2 k)`. For 35 um copper it is 0.6 to 3.8 mK on the harness boards (at most 0.005 % of the rise). Figure: `fig_01_section_profile.png` of `research/through-thickness-readout`. Source: derived in `research/through-thickness-readout` (not a published formula); checked on Muzychka, Culham and Yovanovich (2003) case 2 (read), Seri Lee's plate and the independent solver.
