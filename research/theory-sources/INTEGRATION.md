## 2. Findings about the program, with measured impact (proposals; nothing was edited)

**2.1 Horizontal plates: the program's correlations against Raithby and Hollands and Fujii and Imura.** The program's up-face coefficient (`0.54 Ra^(1/4)`, `0.15 Ra^(1/3)` above 1e7, `L = A/P`) is 7 to 26 % above Lienhard's 8.37a with 8.37b for boards of 100 mm and more (Ra 5e3 to 9e5); with radiation of emissivity 0.9 added the **total** coefficient is 3 to 11 % above the source's for 100 x 80 to 300 x 200 mm, so a board would be hotter by up to about that much in the h-limited part of its rise (for 40 x 30 mm the program is 4 % below at 5 K excess and 2 % above at 60 K). The down-face coefficient `0.27 Ra^(1/4)` against Fujii and Imura's 8.38 **on the plate width** (as Lienhard prints it): where 8.38 is valid the program is 5 to 22 % above it on 2 A/P and 28 to 49 % above it on the shorter side (2 to 12 % of the total h of that face); since 8.38 is for a plate with the end effects suppressed, it is expected to be low for a finite board, so the comparison does not decide the down face. *(The first version evaluated 8.38 on A/P and called the down face conservative; the review corrected it, README 6.)*

Options: (a) keep McAdams and state the spread against Raithby and Hollands as a documented limit; (b) adopt 8.37a/b for the up face (a small `fix/` change in `convection.h_natural_horizontal`, with the validation rows of `docs/validation.md` section 22 to be re-run). The effect on a result is the change of the convective share of `h_top`; on the example board it needs a run, which was not made here.

**Reviewer's opinion:** (a). Both forms are textbook correlations of the same data family (McAdams / Lloyd and Moran through Incropera, Raithby and Hollands through Lienhard); a 3 to 11 % difference in the total h is inside the usual scatter of such correlations (Jaffer's comparisons show 1.3 to 3.8 % RMS on one apparatus, and a vertical-plate formula within 1 % of Churchill and Chu's is 4.7 to 16.4 % RMS from the four data sets they used, README 3.1), no measurement in the project prefers one, and the down face moves the other way, so changing only the up face would not make the board estimate clearly better. The question has its own place in roadmap row 13c (C8, the plate level of a horizontal face: an exact or numerical coefficient against both correlations); decide there. Meanwhile document the deviation in the docstring and `docs/theory.md`, and remove any wording that calls the horizontal faces conservative.

**2.2 Mixed convection: `p = 3` against Jaffer and Jaffer's exponents for a horizontal board.** Jaffer and Jaffer (Table 4) find `p = 2` for a level flow over an upward-facing plate, `p = 5` under a downward-facing plate, `sqrt(3) to 3` for a vertical plate in an aiding or opposing flow and 2 for a vertical plate in a level flow. For a hot 100 x 100 mm board (60 C in 20 C air) the program's convective coefficient is 7 to 11 % below the `p = 2` value on the top face and 0.5 to 8 % above the `p = 5` value on the bottom face at 0.2 to 2 m/s (README 3.4). **Reviewer's opinion:** live with it as a limit, without calling it conservative: the two faces move in opposite directions and partly cancel, the exponents were fitted on rough plates in rough or turbulent forced flow with the authors' own natural and forced formulas (a PCB in a fan flow is smooth and laminar), and Churchill (1977) is not read. Changing the exponent needs Churchill (1977) or a second source for the horizontal case, so it is a **research question**, not a change.

**2.3 The thick-plate effect on a heat-sink base (case 2 of Muzychka and others).** On a 10 mm plate of k 150 under 10 x 10 mm sources the FVM, FDM and Analytic are 2.9 to 5.6 K low at the centroids (4 to 7 % of the rise; the Lumped 2.7 to 6.3 K), 67 to 89 % of the bound `q'' t / (2 k)` (3.3 to 8.3 K); the 3D solid is 2.0 to 4.6 K low. The thick-plate warning of the program (`checks.check_thick_plates`, bound above 5 % of the heater rise) would apply: the bound is 5 to 11 % of the 72 to 79 K rise. This confirms the earlier finding (`research/effective-conductivity` 4.6) on a case that was not run there; no change.

**2.4 The grid convergence of the references.** The independent 3D solver is converged in the plane to a thousandth of a kelvin on cell edges and to 0.06 % in the through-thickness sub-slab count at 16 slabs (case 2: 102.419, 102.525, 102.875 K for 16, 8 and 4 slabs, extrapolated 102.373 K against the paper's 102.33 K). The statement of `research/models-comparison` and `research/effective-conductivity` that 2.5 mm cells overstate a 25 mm source by 0.3 to 0.4 K should be re-read with the sub-slab count in mind (a reference with 6 sub-slabs reads 0.1 to 0.4 K high on a 10 mm plate); the program's models are not affected.

## 3. What does not change and why

* `docs/theory.md` equations are not edited: every equation of the program that was checked is the printed one (vertical plate, Hollands exponent, Hashin-Shtrikman, Bruggeman); the horizontal-plate and mixed-convection forms are a choice among published ones (2.1, 2.2), not a misprint.
* The Culham and Yovanovich plateau (Biot above 60) is **not** a program issue: the program does not homogenise a stack, and the plateau remains unexplained (README 3.6).
* The planned topics 12 (Foster), 15 (Gerber import) and 16 (multigrid) keep their plans; 3.8 and 3.9 give the acceptance checks a future implementation must pass (AN11261 Cauer ladder: sum 0.4000 K/W, Zth(4 ms) 0.26 K/W, Foster equivalence to 1e-12; the three Gerber examples; an h-independent V-cycle factor below 0.15).

## 5. Risks

The percentages of 2.1 and 2.2 compare the program with correlations of the open literature that this folder read in a textbook and a preprint, not with a measured board; neither the program nor the references are compared with a physical board anywhere in the project. A change of the horizontal-plate correlation would move every result of boards in still air by up to the amounts above and needs the whole validation battery.
