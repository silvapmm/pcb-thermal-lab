# Text for `docs/research.md` (open investigation: the via group term of the Lumped model)

**A group of vias under a larger footprint in the Lumped model** (`research/lumped-via-groups`, reviewed). The group (cluster) term added by `research/lumped-analytic-vias` was 18 to 32 % too small on dense arrays of the example board; a fixed factor does not fit (the factor that would make the via effect exact is 0.02 to 0.55 on 24 of 36 simple boards, outside 0 to 1 on 8, and the term changes nothing on 4). The hypothesis that the rings already carry the lateral spreading inside the footprint was tested and rejected: the footprint is one node per layer, and without the lateral resistance the micro well alone is up to 62 % too cool. The fault is the series placement: the group term makes every watt of the footprint cross the sideways resistance although the part of the footprint outside the vias has a vertical path of its own. A footprint cut into two zones per layer (the core covered by the vias and the rest), joined by the logarithmic-potential link of the real shapes in parallel with the vertical paths, plus the rise of the rim of the cell of one via and, when the core is hotter than the rest, the bump of the core (added by the review), reduces the mean error of the via effect from 20.9 % (program) and 18.0 % (series group term) to 3.6 % over 36 boards (maximum 9.1 %) on a 0.5 mm raster, 4.5 % on 26 held-out boards, and on the example board with arrays under U1 to -7.1 to -0.1 % (safe side); at the program's 1 mm Lumped raster it is 8.0 % mean (20.8 % max, on the safe side). Limits that stay: a lone via and a row outside the footprint (10 to 30 % on 0.1 to 2 K), elongated footprints and clusters off the centre of the footprint (to +21 %, flagged by a warning), the 1 mm raster, transients. The published unit cell of Fish (2017, chapter 6, microspreading resistance 1240 K/W) was reproduced to 2 %. Open: a 3D reference with drawn barrels, a Gerber board with many vias, the transient of the zoned network, the raster.
