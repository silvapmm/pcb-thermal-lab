# Text for the project documents (when the proposal is adopted)

**`docs/theory.md`, section on the laminate (1.4 / 4).** The edge film of a cell with copper in every layer used to sit on the copper node at the copper temperature. Two things make that wrong. The laminate near the edge is cooler than the copper: a laminate half-gap of thickness d held at the copper temperature on one face (the middle of the gap is a plane of symmetry) and cooled by a film h_s on its edge carries the share `eta = sum_n 8 / ((2n-1)^2 pi^2) * a_n / (h_s + a_n)`, `a_n = (2n-1) pi sqrt(k_z k_xy) / d`, of the film of a laminate at the copper temperature (0.76 for 3.2 mm and 200 W/m2K). And a node of laminate stands at the centre of its cell, so its film acts behind half a cell of laminate: `g' = g / (1 + h_s dx / (2 k_xy))`. The program therefore cuts the gap into sub-gaps in the cells within `ceil(d / dx) + 1` cells of the board edge when `h_side` > 0, and puts the film of the laminate nodes behind half a cell. Against an independent 3D solver with side films the hottest rise of two-layer boards 0.8 to 3.2 mm is within 2.2 % up to 200 W/m2K (it was 15.8 % low), at 1.1 to 1.9 times the unknowns on a 30 x 20 mm board. Source status: separation of variables (Carslaw and Jaeger) and the finite-volume boundary condition (Patankar) from memory; checked by an independent finite-volume solution and the 3D solver, not by a published case. Figures: `research/laminate-edge-band/figures/fig_01`, `fig_02`, `fig_03`.

**`docs/validation.md`.** Table: error of the hottest rise against the 3D reference, program as is and with the rule, by thickness, `h_side` and cell (README 3 and 6 of `research/laminate-edge-band`).

**`docs/models.md` limits.** With an edge coefficient above 500 W/m2K on boards 3.2 mm thick the FVM and FDM read 5 % or more low with the four sub-gaps of the default laminate; use `laminate_n_max` 8 or 16.
