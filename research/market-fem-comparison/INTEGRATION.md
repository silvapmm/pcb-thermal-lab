# Plan: comparing the program's models with market solvers (proposal; the program is not touched)

Nothing here changes `pcbthermal/`, tests, examples or the project documents. The work stays in `research/` and its results feed `docs/validation.md` only after the usual review and the maintainer's decision.

## 1. Proposed steps (one heavy job at a time; scratch environments outside the repository)

1. **Case file** (1 to 2 h): one JSON description per case, generated by the existing builders of `research/independent-fem-extensions` (hollow barrel, part over a via cluster with a plate, stripes, L outline, transient) and the read-out points of Figure 2 (part centre, barrel foot, board edge, T_min, edge heat, heat balance).
2. **Mesher** (about 6 h, shared): Gmsh, scripted in Python in the scratch environment, one geometry written once for FEniCSx and CalculiX (same nodes, different element orders). Gmsh used as an executable if the maintainer prefers to keep the GPL library out of the research process.
3. **FEniCSx adapter** (6 to 8 h) with tests: closed-form checks (a slab with a film, an energy balance) before any board; then the four steady cases; then the transient (5 to 6 h more).
4. **CalculiX adapter** (7 to 10 h) with the same tests; the plate/via-cluster case and the L outline first.
5. **Report** (3 to 4 h): table of the read-out points per solver and case, difference to our scikit-fem reference and to the program's five models, one figure per case; the README states the mesh levels and the unknowns. Then the reviewer; then the maintainer's decision on `docs/validation.md`.
6. **Optional** after step 5: Elmer or OpenFOAM only if the first two disagree; one SimScale Community run if the maintainer opens an account (public project).

Total for steps 1 to 5: about 35 to 50 hours of agent work (estimate), peak memory of one solve below about 3 GB (estimate; to be measured and refused above a stated budget as the extensions folder does), disk 4 to 8 GB for the conda environment.

## 2. Risks

* The conda route of DOLFINx and the third-party Homebrew tap of CalculiX can break on a new macOS; keep the exact versions printed in the README.
* Thin features (the 25 um hollow barrel wall, the copper layers of 18 to 70 um) need local mesh levels; a coarse mesh would make the 'independent' result wrong for the same reason our own is hard. Mesh convergence must be shown (three levels) before any agreement is claimed.
* Anisotropic laminate and per-element conductivity differ in each tool's input; adapters must be checked on a slab case with a known answer first.
* A match proves discretisation, not physics (README 1). No sentence may suggest validation against a real board.

## 3. Decisions for the maintainer

1. Go or no-go for the comparison, and whether the two recommended codes are FEniCSx and CalculiX (the author's recommendation).
2. Gmsh as an imported GPL module in the scratch process, or only as an executable (the safe route, one hour more).
3. Whether to try SimScale Community (public project, free-tier thermal limited) as a third outside check.
4. No commercial solver in committed numbers (Ansys academic terms forbid benchmarking, COMSOL forbids publishing trial results); a licence the maintainer already owns may be used for a private check, with numbers not committed unless its EULA is read and allows it.
5. Disk and scratch-environment budget (4 to 8 GB) and a memory ceiling for these solves.
