# Research summary: Gerber import, nets and the mapping of copper to the grid

Ready to paste into the project `docs/research.md` as a section "Open now: Gerber import..." when the maintainer adopts the work (updated 2026-10-23). Everything is reproducible from `research/gerbers/` (README sections 4.x, `CONCLUSIONS.md`, `LOG.md`); nothing in `pcbthermal/` was changed.

## Open now: Gerber import, nets and the mapping of copper to the grid (2026-10-23)

**Question.** A Gerber is vector data; the FVM works on square cells and knows only the copper *fraction* of each cell (and the fill of a zone). Can the copper be read without third-party licences, how should it feed the grid, what does it do in each model, and can polygons be tied to nets?

**Answers** (numbers in `research/gerbers/README.md`):

* **Reading: done without libraries.** `lib/native_gerber.py` (NumPy only) draws copper layers and reads drills: same raster as gerbonara + shapely at the pixel, 149 of 164 comparable files of gerbonara's test set within 3 %, drills identical to gerbonara on 35 of 35 files (after fixing the Excellon zero options and four more defects), X2 attributes (file function, aperture functions, net names) read. It refuses what it cannot do (aperture blocks, mirror/rotation/scale, moire, a drill file without a unit).
* **Resolution.** Pixel at most half the thinnest track; a coverage (grey-level) mask at 0.1 mm cuts the error of the flow-based faces from 6.5 to 4.1 % on 21 real windows (3 to 4 times on thin tracks); a 45° track on the lattice can be ±6 % in area, a track as wide as the pixel can vanish; 8-connected labelling keeps a thin track in one piece; 8 to 10 pixels across are needed for the resistance of a thin tilted track.
* **The fill fraction is biased cool**: 10 to 23 % on real copper (up to 49 % across stripes). Flow-based face conductances (Sampson 2005/2017, prior art) are within 0.3 to 3.6 % in every case tested at 5.5 s per 100 x 160 mm layer; lambda 0.5 (geometric mean of the Wiener bounds per direction) 1.0 to 3.2 % at no cost; Blackmore's tile method (2010) is the lower bound plus the arithmetic fraction and alone is 2.6 to 9.5 % hot. No single mapping wins in every regime (a plane that stops short of fixed posts: the fill fraction is best without convection).
* **`Zone.fill` keeps its formula; imported copper needs its own element.** A zone is one polygon without holes and zones are combined per cell with a maximum, so copper tiled in many polygons loses copper in every shared cell (+7 % FVM to +17 % FDM, test); the hatched-zone fill would move by +26 to +45 % with a geometric mean. One line of the program (an additive union) is advisable independently.
* **Every model** (README 4.16, 4.27): through a copy of the program with the additive union, FVM 11.9 to 2.6 % (mean heater rise against the resolved sheet), FDM 15.9 to 7.1 %, Lumped 13.0 to 4.8 % (it reads the FVM conductance), Analytic 18.6 to 12.2 % and 5.9 % with one effective conductance per layer (heater-influence-weighted geometric-type mean). The 3D solid is not usable as a check (already +43 % hot, strongly dependent on the cell size; to be checked by the maintainer). Only the FVM has the directional-face prototype.
* **Vias and nets** (README 4.20, 4.29): holes from the drill file (plated or not from the X2 comment) matched to pads by position, aperture function (`ViaPad`, `ComponentPad`) and net; on a real 4-layer KiCad board 225 of 225 vias have the same net on all four layers. Zone fills often carry no net, so the net of a piece of copper is taken by connectivity. `lib/joule.py` gives the current and Joule heat of a net as power per cell (sum exactly I²R); missing for a feature: current per net or pad, vias between layers, rho(T) coupling, the interface.
* **Literature** (`docs/literature.md`): the flow-based faces are Sampson's method, the tile bound is Blackmore's, Guenin suggests two tests not yet done (local vertical overlap of a smeared layer, copper stopping before the posts at board scale); the three articles and the Ucamco format page were read through texts supplied by the maintainer, the Gerber specification not at all.
* **Import end to end** (README 4.31, `lib/importer.py`): files, vias, nets and face conductances through the program's FVM; heater error -15.1 % today against -2.1 / -5.3 % (Stormduino) and -5.0 % against +4.0 / +2.1 % (own board); another reader changes it by 0.01 to 0.04 %. Missing: layer/outline confirmation, several heaters, faces in the FDM and 3D solid.
* **Nets against a KiCad project** (README 4.33): Stormduino Gerbers + `.kicad_pcb`: 1380/1380 track points, 48/48 via points and 281/281 pad points on our copper; no piece or joined group with two nets; 55 of 56 nets without a zone fill split like the CAD file. **Our board in the five models** (4.34): cell mappings +8 to +13 % (FVM, FDM), +35 % (Lumped). **Rectangle board** (4.35): Lumped -16 %, Analytic -26 % against the FVM; the 3D solid is wrong on a single copper layer (no laminate under it), +8 % with two.
* **Our own board** (`lib/our_board.py`, README 4.30): an IC with circular tracks, eight nets, thermal vias, a ring and a drill file, generated by the repository (no licence issue); a test bench and the source of figures 04 to 06.

**Not validated**: no measurement of a physical board; the 2D Bruggeman and Hashin-Shtrikman closed forms and the Keller-Dykhne duality are from memory (checked by limits); Sampson's board could not be rebuilt (an analogue reproduces the mechanism, a factor 5 to 10 where the article has 26).

**Open decisions for the maintainer**: which stage enters the program (`INTEGRATION.md`); the zone `pattern` for hatched zones; the additive union of zones; a repeat of the mapping studies with finer pixels or the nine-point stencil; the Ucamco test files and example data sets as extra conformance material (download by the maintainer, never committed).
