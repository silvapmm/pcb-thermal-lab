# Text for `docs/theory.md` section 8 (natural convection of a vertical face), to use when adopted

The coefficient of a vertical isothermal plate rests on the similarity solution of the laminar boundary layer: with `eta = (y/x)(Gr_x/4)^(1/4)` the equations `f''' + 3 f f'' - 2 f'^2 + theta = 0`, `theta'' + 3 Pr f theta' = 0` give the local Nusselt number `Nu_x = -theta'(0) (Gr_x/4)^(1/4)` (Ostrach, NACA Report 1111, Table I: `-theta'(0)` = 0.5046 at Pr 0.72, 0.5671 at 1) and, because the local coefficient falls as `x^(-1/4)`, a plate average of 4/3 of the local value at `L`: `Nu = C(Pr) Ra^(1/4)` with `C(0.71) = 0.5157` (exact, own solver). The term `0.670 Ra^(1/4)/[1+(0.492/Pr)^(9/16)]^(4/9)` of the laminar form of Churchill and Chu is this coefficient to 0.3 % (0.5142 at Pr 0.71); the added 0.68 is the leading-edge term (the layer is not thin at the foot of the plate). A 2D numerical solution of a finite thin plate (own solver) lies 2.2 % (`Ra` 1e4) to 0.6 % (3e7) below the laminar form; the all-range form joins the laminar and the turbulent regimes and is 5 % below the numerical plate at 1e4 to 1e5 and 6 to 10 % above it at 1e7 to 3e7; `0.59 Ra^(1/4)` (empirical, textbooks) is 14 % above the exact coefficient and 4 to 12 % above the numerical plate. Sources and status: `research/plate-h-level/README.md` 2 to 6.
