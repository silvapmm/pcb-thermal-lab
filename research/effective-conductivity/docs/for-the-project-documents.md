* `docs/validation.md` section 15 (and 17), `docs/theory.md` section 7 and `research/block-and-fins/`, `research/component-heatsink/`: the numerical example of the spreading resistance of a plate (aluminium 100 x 100 x 1.3 mm, source 25 x 25 mm, `R0` 1.0 K/W: centre 0.66 K/W) is in the Electronics Cooling article *Calculating Spreading Resistance in Heat Sinks* by **Seri Lee** (January 1998), not in a paper of "Lee, Song, Au and Moran (1995)", which has not been located. * Where the documents say that the effective conductivity of a board can be taken as the geometric mean of the series and parallel conductivities, or that the harmonic mean is adequate (Lemczyk and others), add that Culham and Yovanovich (1998) show errors up to 70 % to 300 % for any such mean because of the spreading resistance and the layer order.

## Text for `docs/validation.md` (a new section)

**Published cases reproduced with an independent 3D solver.** `research/effective-conductivity/` (README, twenty figures counting the diagrams). An independent finite-volume solver of a laminated block (`lib/layered3d.py`) reproduces: the centre, edge-midpoint and corner constriction resistance of Seri Lee's plate (0.668, 1.284 and 2.275 K/W against 0.66, 1.29 and 2.38); the cross of five sources of Muzychka, Culham and Yovanovich (maximum 80.62 C against 80.61, area-weighted mean source temperature 78.90 against 78.87) and their first case within 1 % of the rise (the centroid temperatures between the paper's two solutions); the effective conductivity of the five layer orders of Culham and Yovanovich within 2 to 6 %. The five models of the program agree with it on the Cu / FR4 / Cu version of that coupon for source areas of 1 % to 100 % of the board (FVM, 3D solid and Analytic within 0.2 %, FDM within 1.2 % for a 5 mm source on 50 mm, Lumped within 0.3 %). One temperature node through the thickness is the mean of the layer: the surface under a source is hotter by at most `q'' t / (2 k)`, less when the heat spreads sideways (measured 1.6 % of the rise for a 10 mm plate of `k` 50 and 8 to 9 % for `k` 10, against bounds of 2.25 % and 14 to 16 %; `research/effective-conductivity/` 4.6), negligible for copper layers. The Biot plateau of 4.8 W/mK in Figure 5 of Culham and Yovanovich cannot be reached by the stated stack: its top FR4 layer alone has 2.1 K/W under the source, the plateau needs 0.62 K/W.
