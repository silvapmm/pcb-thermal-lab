# Text for the project documents (when the work is adopted)

**docs/research.md / docs/versions.md 'Planned'.** Swing of a fast power wave (investigated in `research/fast-wave-oscillation/`, reviewed in its README 8): one node per copper layer carries the capacity of about 0.77 mm of laminate (1394 J/m2K against 120 J/m2K of the copper), so for a wave whose shortest stretch (on or off time) is below about 4 s the FVM and FDM understate the swing at the part (2 to 3 times at 1 to 3 Hz, 8 to 12 times from 50 Hz to 1 kHz on 1.6 mm boards, checked against two independent references that agree within 2 %), the average-or-resolve decision averages a wave above the budget in 20 % of the decisions of a sweep (25 % at the 2 mm cell the application uses for the step response), and the curve of a heater that is resolved shows the same small ripple (0.74 K against 6.4 K). Proposed together: (A) the closed-form impedance of the layered stack under the part (two-sided for an inner layer) as a floor of the decision where the heater's layer has copper under the part; (B) a heater faster than the node can follow is averaged in the board run and its curve and peak get the closed-form ripple; (D) the step response at the cell of the run. A floor alone is not consistent (the decision and the curve disagree) and makes the example board's run exceed the step limit. Left: a part on bare laminate (2.2 times low between 3 and 50 Hz, a warning) and a part near the board edge at slow waves.

**docs/validation.md (model limits).** The layered models understate the swing of a wave whose shortest stretch is below about 4 s (one and two layers; about 2 s on four layers of a 1.6 mm board); measured against an axisymmetric modal reference and a Cartesian 3D frequency-domain reference with the copper and the laminate resolved through the thickness (README 2 and 8).
