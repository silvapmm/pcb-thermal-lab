# Text for `docs/research.md` (summary, to use while the work is not implemented)

**The channel effect between close fins (research/fin-channel).** The finned metal block of the program uses one coefficient on every face of the fins and warns only below a 4 mm gap. The Elenbaas channel (two vertical isothermal plates) says that the coefficient between the fins falls as the gap closes: the Nusselt number `Nu_b = h b / k` goes from `0.59 Ra_b^(1/4)` (open) to `Ra_b / 24` (closed), with `Ra_b = g beta theta b^4 Pr / (nu^2 L)`; the composite of Bar-Cohen and Rohsenow (`[(24/Ra_b)^2 + (0.59 Ra_b^(1/4))^-2]^(-1/2)`) is the form used. What was done: equations 8, 20, 21 and 22 of the Waterloo review read; reproduced against an independent marching solution of the channel (inside the envelope of the four correlations to 3 %, `Ra_b/24` within 2 % at `Ra_b` = 1); the optimum spacing (`2.7156 L / Ra_L^(1/4)`, `Ra_b` = 54.4, `Nu_b` = 1.308 derived from equation 21) and the shape of `Q(s)` of published arrays reproduced from a 6 mm gap up; the program's block run with the channel coefficient on a copy (40 mm channels, 3 W): the channel effect raises the rise by +1.9 % at a 6.5 mm gap, +4.7 % at 5.5 mm and +11.8, +27, +55 % at 4.5, 3.5, 2.5 mm, more for longer channels and cooler blocks; a warning on `Ra_b < 90` (that is, `phi < 0.9`) misses 1 of the 667 grid cases where the channel costs 10 % of the finned face, the 4 mm rule misses 367. Open: arrays open in front (3 mm), horizontal boards, radiation between fins, a catalogue with dimensions, a measured array of trustworthy level. Decisions: `research/fin-channel/INTEGRATION.md` section 6.
