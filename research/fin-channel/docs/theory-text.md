# Text for `docs/theory.md` section 7 (fins) and for the warning, to use when the work is adopted

**Close fins (channel effect).** The fin formula of this section assumes one coefficient on every face. Between fins closer than a few millimetres the air in the channel is slowed: two vertical isothermal plates at a gap `b` and length `L` have, in the composite of Bar-Cohen and Rohsenow (1984), `Nu_b = h b / k = [(24/Ra_b)^2 + (0.59 Ra_b^(1/4))^(-2)]^(-1/2)` with `Ra_b = g beta theta b^4 Pr / (nu^2 L)`; the first term is the fully developed limit (`Nu_b = Ra_b / 24`: the gap is full of warm air and the coefficient falls with the cube of the gap), the second the isolated plate. The fraction of the isolated-plate limit that the channel keeps is `phi = [1 + (14.16 / Ra_b^(3/4))^2]^(-1/2)` (0.9 at `Ra_b` = 90, 0.5 at 16.5). The heat of an array of fixed width has a maximum at the optimum gap `b = 2.72 L / Ra_L^(1/4)` (the channel Rayleigh number is then 54), observed at 7 to 12 mm for 100 to 500 mm fins on vertical bases. Limits: vertical channels with a closed front; narrow gaps of arrays open in front carry more heat than this model gives; horizontal boards are not covered; radiation between the fins is not included.

**Warning text (proposal A).** "{name}: the channel between the fins ({gap} mm wide, about {L} mm long, block {theta} K above the air) has a channel Rayleigh number of {Ra_b}; the air between the fins cools {loss} % less than a plate of the same height ({phi} of its coefficient). Use a larger pitch, shorter channels, or the datasheet theta_SA. Valid for a vertical board; for a horizontal board the effect is not quantified."
