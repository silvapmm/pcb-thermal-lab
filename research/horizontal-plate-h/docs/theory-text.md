**Horizontal faces.** A board with `Board.orientation = "horizontal"` has the hot face of layer 1 looking up and the last layer looking down. The coefficients are the textbook correlations of an isolated horizontal plate with the characteristic length `L = A/P` (area over perimeter of one face; `A/P` is a quarter of the side of a square, half the width of a long strip, half the radius of a disc): upper face `Nu = 0.54 Ra^(1/4)` up to `Ra = 1e7` and `0.15 Ra^(1/3)` above (the measurements of Lloyd and Moran, in which all plan forms collapse with `A/P`), lower face `Nu = 0.27 Ra^(1/4)`. A face colder than the air swaps the two.

**What they are worth** (`research/horizontal-plate-h/`). Over the Rayleigh numbers of the boards (`Ra` of `A/P` 1e3 to 5e5) the upper-face correlation is 3 to 7 % below the fit of Jaffer (2023) to the same measurements, inside their scatter (about 5 %). The lower face has a different exponent in the published forms (Schulenberg's theory and Jaffer's fit go as `Ra^(1/5)`, with a conduction term in Jaffer's) and depends on the shape through the distance from the middle line of the plate to its edge (half the shorter side of a rectangle, which is `2 A/P` for a square), which `A/P` does not carry: the program's value is equal to the published square at `Ra` 1e3 and 15 to 43 % above it from 1e4 to 3e5, and 29 % below the published long strip at 1e3 and equal at 1e5. Both correlations belong to an isolated face (the back insulated or flush in an insulating surround); on a thin board with both faces hot, the warm air of the lower face rises at the edges into the inflow of the upper face, which a steady symmetric numerical solution (strip and disc) shows as a fall of the upper-face coefficient that the correlations do not contain, and whose size is not established because the same solution does not reproduce the measured isolated upper face. The rise of a board changes by less than 3 % with the published lower-face forms instead of the program's (4 % without radiation) and would change by 2 to 10 % with radiation (up to 21 % without) if the upper face lost 40 % of its coefficient.
