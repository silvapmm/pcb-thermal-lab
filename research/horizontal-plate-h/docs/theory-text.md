**Horizontal faces.** A board with `Board.orientation = "horizontal"` has the hot face of layer 1 looking up and the last layer looking down. The coefficients are the textbook correlations of an isolated horizontal plate with the characteristic length `L = A/P` (area over perimeter of one face; `A/P` is a quarter of the side of a square, half the width of a long strip, half the radius of a disc): upper face `Nu = 0.54 Ra^(1/4)` up to `Ra = 1e7` and `0.15 Ra^(1/3)` above (the measurements of Lloyd and Moran, in which all plan forms collapse with `A/P`), lower face `Nu = 0.27 Ra^(1/4)`. A face colder than the air swaps the two.

**What they are worth** (`research/horizontal-plate-h/`, reviewed). Over the Rayleigh numbers of the boards (`Ra` of `A/P` 1e3 to 5e5) the published upper-face levels lie on both sides of the program's: the fit of Jaffer (2023) is 3 to 8 % above it, the form of Raithby and Hollands (as printed by Lienhard, equation 8.37) from 1 % above to 20 % below it. The published lower-face forms (Schulenberg's theory, Fujii and Imura on the width of a two-dimensional plate, Kadambi and Drake for a disc, Jaffer's fit) go as `Ra^(1/5)`, not `Ra^(1/4)`, and depend on the shape through the distance from the middle line of the plate to its edge (half the shorter side of a rectangle, `2 A/P` for a square), which `A/P` does not carry: the program's lower face equals the published square at `Ra` 1e3 and is 16 to 43 % above it from 1e4 to 3e5 (more cooling than the published forms), and is 29 % below the published long strip at 1e3 and equal to it at 1e5. Neither face is shown to be conservative. With the published forms instead of the program's, the rise of the hottest point of the boards studied (the two examples, square boards of 40 to 200 mm, the finned block with its fins down) changes by -2.7 to +1.3 % with radiation (the default) and by -3.8 to +3.3 % with the emissivity at zero. Both correlations belong to an isolated face (the back insulated or flush in an insulating surround); on a thin board with both faces hot, the warm air of the lower face rises at the edges into the inflow of the upper face. A steady symmetric two-dimensional solution (strip and disc) shows this as a fall of the upper-face coefficient to 0.56 to 0.70 of the isolated face's value; its size in a real board is not established, because the upper-face flow of a real board is unsteady and three-dimensional and the same solution does not reproduce the measured isolated upper face. If the upper face lost 40 %, the rise would be 2 to 10 % higher with radiation (5 to 21 % without). Not modelled: the coupling of the two faces, the shape of the lower face, the unsteady plume of the upper face.
