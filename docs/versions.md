# Version history

What was implemented in every version (with the module where it lives and how it is tested) and what we still want to implement (last chapter, "Planned"). The release notes are `CHANGELOG.md`; the physics is in `models.md` and `theory.md`, the numbers in `validation.md`. **Newest first.** Dates are those of the release tags. Each bullet reads: feature (code; tests).

## Unreleased (2.5.0, on `develop`)

* **Analytic edge transfer** (`fix/analytic-edge-transfer`; `analytic.py`, `model.py` `equivalent_rectangle`; theory `theory.md` 6; validation `validation.md` 4.1 and 13; study `research/models-comparison/scripts/study_analytic_edge_transfer.py`; tests `test_analytic_edge.py`). The edge convection `h_side` is moved to the faces of each layer as `h_side x perimeter x slab / (W H)` (the same split by layer as the FVM edge conductance), in the steady sheet, the correction of vias and heatsinks and the transient; the perimeter is that of the real outline (`board.edge_perimeter`, set on the copy that `equivalent_rectangle` makes). The heat through the edge is `power_out["sides"]`. A new warning measures the share of the power the transfer puts in the wrong place (above 1 %) and gives the direction (reads low or high). Bit-identical without `h_side`. Mean absolute error against the FVM on 90 uniform boards 38.2 % to 4.5 %; seven 100 x 80 mm boards with the part at the centre are about 1 % worse. The Lumped model is untouched. The frozen records that moved are the pending file `analytic-edge.json` (fold and delete at the 2.5.0 refreeze).
* **Natural convection: `laminar` for new projects and a low-Rayleigh warning** (`fix/natural-convection-low-ra`; proposal A and warning (a) of `research/plate-h-level`; theory `theory.md` 8; validation `validation.md` 22). `Board.natural_form` keeps `general` as the class value and for project files written before (same numbers); new projects take `laminar` from `defaults.json` (Preferences, Environment panel). Against the 2D numerical plate the laminar form is closer at Ra 1e3 to 1e4 and on low and tall plates; vertical computed-h boards move -0.1 to -2 % of the rise on small boards and up to +0.3 % on large ones, the example board turned vertical -0.22 K (-0.36 % of the rise) in every model. `checks.check_low_rayleigh` (called once per run from `api._part_warnings`, reference model; forced-air faces excluded; horizontal boards not covered) warns below a face Rayleigh number of 1e3. The planned item on the channel Rayleigh number is dropped: the fin warning (`Ra_b < 90`) covers the channel. Tests: `test_natural_form.py`, `test_model_limit_warnings.py`. Not covered: no study between Ra 1e3 and 1e4, no 3D reference of the convection.
* **Gerber reader** (`feature/gerber-reader`; `pcbthermal/gerber/`: `parser.py`, `macros.py`, `excellon.py`, `outline.py`, `raster.py`, `functions.py`, `archive.py`; theory `theory.md` 1.6; models `models.md` 19; evidence `research/gerber-real-boards`, `research/gerber-import`; tests `test_gerber_reader.py`, `test_gerber_spec.py`, `test_gerber_conformance.py`, `test_gerber_drill_outline.py`, `test_gerber_detect_archive.py`). Our own reader, no new dependency and **no interface yet** (no import into a project, no GUI): RS-274X with X2 (macros with arithmetic and no `eval`, regions, arcs G74/G75, step and repeat, polarity, the attributes `TO.C`, `TO.N`, `TO.P` kept apart), Excellon, XNC and X2 drill layers with the plating and slots, the outline chained and nested by the even-odd rule, a one-byte coverage raster by tiles, the pixel proposal and the sample-spacing warning (error `s / w` of one feature, 50 % in the worst case on the thinnest feature at the suggested pixel, no mean bias; the warning fires below two spacings), the layer-function detector (X2, then the drill header, then the name tables) and a safe ZIP reader. Features that would change the geometry are refused with a message naming file and feature. Hostile input is bounded (`MAX_RASTER_PIXELS` 400 million, `MAX_OPERATIONS` 2 million, ZIP limits): 0 raw errors in 1100 mutated files and 300 damaged ZIPs. Memory: 1 byte per stored pixel per layer, a working set per tile of about 90 bytes per sub-pixel capped near 377 MB, 600 bytes per operation at the parse peak. Deviation from the 2026 specification on purpose: G37 resets G01 (the Ucamco `example_level_holes` needs it). Review: copper areas of 109 files within -0.026 to +0.18 % of the exact vector areas; drills identical hole by hole on 6 boards. Open for the maintainer: `MAX_MEMBER_BYTES` 1 GB against a proposed 256 MB, and the suggested pixel at half the thinnest feature against a quarter.
* **Parts from the paste layers and X3 components** (`feature/paste-parts`; `pcbthermal/gerber/parts.py`, `pcbthermal/gerber/components.py`, `model.ImportPart.from_file`; theory `theory.md` 1.8; models `models.md` 19.2; evidence `research/gerber-real-boards` 9 to 12; tests `tests/test_gerber_parts.py`, `tests/test_gerber_components.py`). **No interface yet.** (1) `parts.py`: placement files (`read_placement`, `parse_placement`; KiCad, Altium and generic CSV, header words and units, 64 MB limit), the paste openings (`read_openings`: the layer is drawn cluster by cluster at 0.05 mm, objects whose function is `Profile` or `NonMaterial` are left out, a negative image is refused), the envelope table (`envelope`: 0402 to 1206, SOIC, SOT-223, pin headers, QFN from the name), the matching rule (`match_envelope`), the bottom-side convention and mirror (`bottom_convention`, `bottom_mirror`), the frame check (`frame_check`, constants `FRAME_MIN_GAIN`, `FRAME_MIN_PARTS`, `FRAME_MIN_BEST`, `FRAME_MIN_SHIFT_MM`, `FRAME_MISFIT`), the heat-source shape (`shape_polygon`: `hull` default, `box`, `largest`), the click (`opening_at`), `build_parts` (X3, then `TO.C`, then position, then paste only) and the re-import merge (`diff_parts`, `merge_parts`). * **Project file format 2 for projects with imported boards** (`feature/project-format-2`; `project.py` `FORMAT_2`, `format_number`, `read_project_json`, `_write_text_atomic`, `_backup_path`; `model.py` `ImportRecord`, `ImportFile`, `ImportPart`, `ImportedCopper`; tests `tests/test_project_format2.py` and the stress scenario in `tests/test_gui_stress.py`; documented in `models.md` 8.1). The problem is nested under a `project` key beside an `imports` list and the file is written only when the project has imports; any other project stays format 1, byte-identical to the earlier versions. The released 2.4.x answers "Cannot open file" (`KeyError('board')`) and never overwrites a format 2 file (checked against v2.4.0, v2.4.2 and v2.4.5), so no 2.4.6 patch is needed. A newer format is refused with the writer's version; unknown keys are kept on save; `load_problem` reads format 2. Every save is atomic (temporary file, `fsync`, `os.replace`; permissions and symbolic link kept; a read-only file refused) and keeps `.bak`, `.bak2`, ... copies when it changes the format of the file or the old file is not JSON. Nothing in the models reads the imports yet: the importer, the imported copper and the parts come in the next branches of the Gerber work.

* **Imported copper** (`feature/imported-copper`; `pcbthermal/copper.py`; theory `theory.md` 1.7; models `models.md` 19.1; validation `validation.md` 27; evidence `research/gerber-real-boards` 4.1, 10 and the study `scripts/study_program_cells.py`; tests `tests/test_imported_copper.py`). The coverage masks of an import (`Problem.imported`, one byte per pixel and layer, kept as packed text in the import record of project format 2; `model.ImportedCopper` gains `mapping`) become the copper of the board: an imported layer REPLACES the zones of that layer in every model, and the zones drawn there are ignored and warned. Three mappings (`ImportedCopper.mapping`): **faces** (default; `copper.face_conductances`, transmissibility upscaling: one local conduction problem on the pixels between two cell centres per pair of neighbours, FVM and FDM), **lambda** (the geometric mean of the Wiener bounds per cell, much faster, better on 45 degree tracks at about ten pixels per cell) and **fill** (the arithmetic fill, the old path, kept for comparison). **The faces are built on the REDUCED sheet**: the network is built from the cell fill first, so the laminate rule plans its interior laminate nodes on the real copper of each cell and moves a sheet conductance dS out of the copper node of the flagged cells; dS is then taken off every pixel of the cell, copper or bare, before the faces are computed (faces from the unreduced sheet count that laminate twice and are worse than the rule off; a test holds the order). The FVM and FDM take the faces (the FDM windows are centred on the nodes and the edge rows get half faces); the cell fill of the imported layers comes from the masks (box average, `resample_coverage`) for the vertical links, the heat capacity and every other model. The **3D solid** reads the fill (average conductivity) and warns; the **Lumped and Analytic** read only the fill and ALWAYS warn (1 to 16 % too cool in the study, 6 % on average); the FVM and FDM carry one fixed note on the accuracy (and a warning above a 1 mm cell, or below 8 pixels per cell). A cell is almost never a whole number of pixels: the face problems sample the raster at the centres of a grid at least as fine as the raster (`sample_coverage`) instead of averaging it, which keeps the clearances between copper areas (averaging bridged them and read 1.4 to 3.9 % of the rise cool on twelve real windows at cells of 0.92 and 1.09 mm; found by the review, the unaligned-cells defect); `pixels_per_cell` rounds up to an even number, at most `MAX_PIXELS_PER_CELL` = 24 (a finer raster is averaged down). **The import** (`copper.import_copper`, no GUI): rasterises the copper layers one at a time (only the packed text is kept), clears the copper outside a non-rectangular outline, turns plated round holes into vias (slots and unplated holes are said in the warnings) and refuses a panel; the default pixel is `min(m w / 4, cell / 10)` for `m` sub-pixels per pixel and `w` the thinnest feature (a quarter of the thinnest feature, and at most a tenth of the cell, so that the faces have about ten pixels per cell). **Memory** (`memory.py`): `copper_run_bytes` (the unpacked masks held, 1 byte per pixel and layer, and the working set of the face construction, 40 bytes per source pixel of the largest layer plus 50 MB; added to the base of the run), `import_draw_bytes` and `check_import` (refuses BEFORE anything is drawn and names the pixel that fits). **Hostile input** (a project file is hostile): the shape of a mask is checked first (at most 400 million pixels), the text is decoded strictly, the deflated stream is inflated with a cap of the bytes the shape needs plus one (a zip bomb is refused), the extent of the masks must match the board box (a board resized after the import is refused with a message instead of putting the copper in the wrong place), duplicate and out-of-range layer indices are refused. **Saving**: a problem that holds imported copper but no import record (made by `ImportedBoard.to_problem`) used to lose the masks silently on save and reopen as a bare board; it is now written in a record of its own (`Project.run_problem`, `project_to_dict`). `checks.check_input` reads the imported fill and warns on a zone drawn on an imported layer. No interface yet (the import dialog is the next branch, see 'Planned'). **Known limit**: on a 24 mm window the FVM overshoots by +7.9 to +9.6 % at a 2 mm cell (see `validation.md` 27.3 for the cause); the FVM memory law ignores the vias (a separate fix, 'Planned').

* **Memory guard** (`feature/memory-guard`; evidence `research/memory-guard`, reviewed in two rounds; theory `theory.md` 2.7; validation `validation.md` 26). `memory.py`: the physical memory (no dependency; `PCBTHERMAL_MEMORY_BYTES` overrides it), the estimate `base + safety x factor x k1 t_eff^a N (N/N_ref)^(p-1)` per model (FVM, FDM, 3D solid; transient and profile factor 2.0), a cheap lower bound before the exact count, the verdicts ok / slow / refuse against a fraction of the memory (**0.60, a machine preference** `solver.memory_fraction`, not saved in the project; a low *available* memory only warns), the cell that fits and an absolute ceiling of 2 million unknowns for the 3D solid. Replaces `solid3d.MAX_CELLS` and `check_size` (`solid3d.size_limit` keeps its window meaning) and `fvm.MAX_SOR_BLOCK` (the SOR block rule is `75 n^2` bytes summed over the blocks, at most 25 % of the limit). Used by `api.simulate` (a refused 3D solid next to other models is skipped with a warning), `jobs`, the solver panel (verdict next to the unknowns, Run disabled with the reason, Auto cell that fits, the check of an animation in the window), `solid3d.Solid` (its constructor) and the command line (`--memory-fraction`, exit code 3). The animation and the transient keep one LU factor instead of ten (bit-identical frames; peak 1.9 times a steady solve instead of 3.5). The Lumped and the Analytic are not checked. Tests: `tests/test_memory.py`, `test_fvm.py`, `test_gui_stress.py`, `test_help.py`, `test_tooltips.py`.
* **Power Calculator reuses one factorisation** (`fix/inverse-factor-reuse`; `inverse.py` inside `fvm.factor_reuse`; speed only, same results; tests `tests/test_inverse_reuse.py` and `tests/test_maximum_principle.py`). At manual h the runs of the calculator solve one matrix with other powers, so 4 solves become 1 factorisation: bit-identical in 84 of 84 cases, about 3.8 times faster, peak memory equal or lower (example board at 0.5 mm: one call 4.80 to 1.28 s, 679 to 605 MB; the band at design level hand 84.5 to 21.6 s, 2024 to 1952 MB). With computed h the matrix changes at every step, nothing is reused and hashing plus the extra `splu` can cost up to about 17 %. The maximum principle with via couplings is now stated and bounded by a test (16 and 64 positive entries of K for the FVM and FDM on the example board at 1 mm, 832 and 864 at 0.5 mm under a 10 x 10 array); the cold spot on a plane with copper under part of a via footprint is recorded as a limit and a strict expected failure (`theory.md` 2.2; Planned).
* **Lumped zones: the footprint of a part over a dense group of vias in two nodes** (`feature/lumped-zones`; `lumped._zone_network`, `_core_mask`, `_fine_core_share`, `_ZonePeak`, `will_be_zoned`, `cell_for`; `viaconstriction.annulus_out_resistance`, `rim_minus_mean`, `ring_couplings(want_barrel=...)`; `checks.check_lumped_zones`; theory `theory.md` 5.2; validation `validation.md` 18.2; evidence `research/lumped-zones-adoption`, `research/lumped-via-groups`). The cells within `0.7071 d_nn` of the vias of a group in the footprint are the core C, the rest of the footprint is A: two nodes per layer joined by the link of eq. (5.7), the vias at C through the wells of 5.1 (the cluster well kept, capped at 0.75 fin lengths), A to ring 1 by the annulus resistance (5.8), the source split by covered area, capacities as sums of cell capacities, the peak the largest of the field of A, the core plus the rim of the Krogh cell and the core plus the rim plus the bump. The core is painted with the temperature of node C in the map, and the one-node branch has no micro peak (it is the program's code moved, bit for bit). The decision is made on the fine (0.1 mm) count and on the Lumped grid, both at most 92 %, and on the equivalent rectangle for a board with an outline, and passed to the network by `zone_ok` so that the run, the step response and the profile agree. The Lumped raster is **one 0.5 mm cell per board, only when a part will be zoned** (`cell_for`; the other boards keep the 0.5 to 1 mm clip), so the other parts of that board move by the raster too (9 to 18 K on two small parts on bare laminate of a Gerber-like test board, nearer the FVM at 0.5 mm). The profile runner and the step responses use the source split and the zone peak (`_LumpedAdapter.advance`, `step_responses`). Post-run warnings: the bound of the zoned model (up to 16 % of the rise) for an array across the edge, softer texts for off-centre and elongated footprints, a raster coarser than a tenth of the core, and a note when a footprint was kept as one node. Measured: via effect of 36 family boards 10.8 / 31.8 -> 3.8 / 9.1 %, 26 held-out 18.2 / 108.9 -> 4.6 / 20.6 %; hottest temperature 5.1 / 14.2 -> 2.6 / 7.2 % and 5.2 / 17.4 -> 1.6 / 7.6 %; the zones are hotter than the one node on three boards of the profile run and on one of the 62 steady boards, on the safe side. Frozen numbers: the Lumped records of `plate_vias` move (140.3555 -> 136.4534 degC, -3.90 K; time to 63 % 27.83 -> 31.00 s) and are held by the pending shift `tests/regression/data/pending/lumped-zones.json`, written by `tests/regression/make_pending.py` and applied by `test_frozen_numbers.py` over `data/v2.4.5.json`, which is not edited: the refreeze of the release folds the shift in and the pending file is **deleted** at that commit (the test fails on a pending file whose base version is older). Tests: `tests/test_lumped_zones.py`, the stress scenario in `tests/test_gui_stress.py`.

* **Lumped via warnings, the unsafe side** (`fix/lumped-via-warning-limits`; `checks.lumped_via_cool_warning`; text only). A part whose via group continues past the footprint edge (within twice the array pitch), stands off its centre by more than 0.1 of the smaller side or lies under a footprint of side ratio above 1.5 warns that the Lumped model can read it too cool (up to about 11 K, a quarter of its rise); the general text now says up to 14 % of the rise too cool and the text of the vias outside every part up to 2.3 times the effect (4 K, 9 % of the rise). * **Laminate options and the face of a part in the profile curves** (`feature/laminate-options`; `laminate.using`, `laminate.parameters`, `SolverOptions.laminate_s_max_mm` and `laminate_n_max`, `profiles` face terms; theory `theory.md` 1.4 and 10; validation `validation.md` 21.2; evidence `research/laminate-nodes`, `research/gerber-real-boards` 10). (1) The two numbers of the laminate rule, the longest sub-gap `S_MAX` (0.05 to 5 mm, default 0.4) and the most sub-gaps `N_MAX` (integer 1 to 16, default 4; 1 turns the cutting off), are options of the run: they are saved in the project only when not the default, `laminate.using()` applies them for the thread so the network builders, the unknown counters and the memory guard see the same numbers, and `api.simulate`, the power calculator and `calibrate` use them. A value such as `8.0` is read as 8, `8.5` is refused. Interface: two spin boxes in the solver panel (greyed with a metal block, where the rule is not applied) and the CLI flags `run --laminate-s-max-mm` and `--laminate-n-max`. A gap gets `min(N_MAX, ceil(d / S_MAX))` sub-gaps, so a larger `N_MAX` alone changes nothing on a gap that `S_MAX` already cuts into `N_MAX` sub-gaps (8 sub-gaps of a 1.6 mm gap need `S_MAX` of 0.19 mm or less, 16 need 0.09 mm or less). (2) The power-profile curves of a part over absent copper in the FVM and FDM carry the quasi-static face term of each power step (`Q t_cu/(2 k_z A)`), and the average-or-resolve decision adds the swing of that term (the power swing times the largest face resistance of the part) to the oscillation of the node, so a fast square wave that the node alone would average can be resolved (6 mm part, 0 to 2 W at 10 ms: peak 187.60 to 189.58 degC). The face is first order: it overstates the swing below about ten times the time constant `tau1` of the half copper layer (0.69, 2.8 and 11 ms for 35, 70 and 140 um). The legend and the summary say hottest at the face of a part where the face is included. Limit: the `transient` command uses the default laminate options. Tests: `tests/test_laminate_options.py`, `tests/test_profile_face.py`.

## 2.4.5 - 2026-11-01: vias, laminate nodes, the SOR and a part across the outline

* **`theory.md` rewritten for the models as implemented** (`docs/theory-rewrite`; evidence `research/theory`). Each model section has one structure (the idea in a picture, unknowns, equations, assembly, solution, limits in words, where it is checked, sources) and the sources are cited by key with their reading status (section 12). Symbols were renamed where one letter had two meanings (`G_mod`, `C_loc` for the Analytic correction `(I + G_mod C_loc) y = y₀`, `N_ring`, `κ` for the wavenumber, `R_c`, `ϕ_ch`, `Θ_part`, ...). The history that the old text carried is here, not in the theory: the weight of a part across the outline was the coverage of the part alone before 2.4.5, then the intersection normalised to the board cover sampled 4 x 4 (`fix/part-across-outline`), and is the plain intersection of an 8 x 8 cover since `fix/board-cover-8x8`; the SOR relaxation factor was `2/(1 + sin(pi/n_max))` before `fix/sor-omega`; the via was one point conductance in the cell of its centre before `fix/point-via`; the Lumped and Analytic models joined the planes through the barrel conductance alone before `fix/via-constriction`; the fixed-point loop of the top path was replaced by a root (`fix/top-path-convergence`); the side walls of a finned block carried the fin ratio before `fix/finned-block-sides`; the limit of the 3D solid was 2 million unknowns before `fix/gui-compute-hang`; a one-layer board had two laminate nodes in every cell before `fix/laminate-nodes` (the Lumped model and the boards with a block still have them). The numbers that the theory quoted are in `validation.md` 25.
* **Frozen-numbers regression test** (`feature/frozen-numbers-test`, 2.4.5; tests only, evidence `research/release-regression-245`). `tests/regression/test_frozen_numbers.py` runs the five models on the 17 boards of the harness (`regress_*.py`, a copy of `research/release-regression-245/lib/`) and compares with `tests/regression/data/v2.4.5.json`, made by `study_freeze_reference.py` from the v2.4.4 baseline and the final 2.4.5 tree (251 records, 58 times to 63 %; the classified diff had 1422 expected shifts and 0 alarms). One tolerance policy: temperatures 1e-8 K, field checksums 1e-10 relative, heat balance 1e-9 W, counts exact, times to 63 % 1e-6 relative. Default 2 mm and the times (about 20 s); `PCBT_FROZEN_CELLS=2,1,0.5` for the release procedure. The expected-shift table of the release is `research/release-regression-245/reference/expected_shifts_2.4.5.json`.
* **One factorisation for the top-path split** (`fix/top-path-speed`, 2.4.5; speed only, bit-identical). `fvm.factor_reuse()` (a context, one LU per thread, released when the outermost block ends) and `fvm.direct_solve(A, b)` (spsolve outside the block; inside it `splu` keyed by a hash of the CSC structure and values, the old LU released before a new one is made); `fvm.Network.solve_steady` and `fdm.Network.solve_steady` call it, and `api._solve_with_tc` opens the block around the power-coefficient loop and the top-path split. ### Fixes waiting for a study

* **Spatial edge correction of the Analytic model** (after `fix/analytic-edge-transfer`): the transfer assumes the edge at the mean board temperature, so the error has the sign of the warned share (14 % low with the parts far from the edge, 37 % high with a part at the edge, at 200 W/m2K). A prototype that treats the edge strips as local heatsinks in the Green-function correction took 100 x 80 mm boards from +37 % / -12 % to -0.2 to -2.8 %, but left -1 to -11 % on 30 x 20 mm boards (`research/models-comparison/scripts/study_analytic_edge_transfer.py`); it needs a decision and the study of the small boards before it enters.
* **Laminate edge efficiency in the FVM and FDM** (found by `fix/analytic-edge-transfer`, to investigate): the edge film of a whole layer slab hangs on the copper node, so the laminate between two copper layers loses heat through its edge as if it were at the copper temperature; against the independent 3D solver the FVM reads 13 to 17 % low at `h_side` 200 W/m2K on thick boards (up to 1.4 % at 10 W/m2K on 3.2 mm; under 0.4 % on 1.6 mm boards at the default). Moves numbers; investigate first.
* **`info["convention"]` of parts matched by `TO.C`** (found by the review of `feature/paste-parts`; small fix, no result changes): for parts found by the designator in the paste file, `build_parts` reports `info["convention"]` as 'as is' by default without having checked the placement file for the bottom-side sign convention; it should say 'not checked' (or run the check) so that the import dialog does not present an unchecked file as verified. Tests: `tests/test_gerber_parts.py`.

* **Cut cells treated by their real shape** (option P1 of `research/rotated-square-3d`, after the part across the outline): a cell cut by the outline conducts with its cover, so a part over a cut edge reads 0.5 to 0.7 K (about 1 % of the rise) hotter at 1 to 0.5 mm than with the edge on the grid lines; the remedy is a cut-cell scheme (conductances from the real shape of the cell). Maintainer: 2.6.0 (briefly in the 2.5.0 scope, moved back); evidence: `docs/validation.md` section 23.
* **Laminate nodes, what is left** (after `fix/laminate-nodes`, 2.5.0): cell-major ordering of the unknowns (its own item below); a warning when the copper detail is finer than the cell (the Gerber import); the time of the top-path split with computed coefficients (its own item below). * **Node prototype of the top path** (housekeeping, `research/component-heatsink/scripts/package_prototype.py`): it rebuilds `net.g_amb` without `g_pin` and applies the bottom film to `g_bottom[-1]`, which is now a laminate node; use `net.n_board - 1` and `res.extras['bottom_face_mean']`; check `chain_study.py` too.

* **Via footprint weights on a plane with copper only under part of the footprint** (2.5.0, found by the review of `fix/inverse-factor-reuse`, to investigate first; `docs/theory.md` 2.2): the weights of a via on each plane are fixed, so where the copper covers only part of the footprint the via takes heat from cells that cannot supply it and the FVM shows a cold spot, and an exaggerated via effect. Examples (two-layer board, h = 10 W/m2K, 2 W part of 3 x 3 mm, copper of the top layer ending through the centre of a 0.3 mm via): FVM cold spot of -7.9 K at 0.5 mm and -14.4 K at 0.25 mm, none in the FDM; a part on bare laminate over half a via reads below the ambient, -2.5 % of the largest rise at h = 10 W/m2K to -38 % at 5000 W/m2K; the via effect on the part is 7.7 K in the FVM at 0.25 mm (FDM 11 K) against 2.9 K for the 3D solid at 0.5 mm.
