# Version history

What was implemented in every version (with the module where it lives and how it is tested) and what we still want to implement (last chapter, "Planned"). The release notes are `CHANGELOG.md`; the physics is in `models.md` and `theory.md`, the numbers in `validation.md`. **Newest first.** `docs/version-comparison.md` runs the same 17 boards on every version from v2.0.0 to the tree: what each version implemented, why the numbers moved and the error of each model against an independent 3D solver (regenerated, reviewer pending). Dates are those of the release tags. Each bullet reads: feature (code; tests).

---

## Unreleased (2.5.0, on `develop`)

* **Mirror image of a board edge in the via well** (`feature/via-edge-image`, adopts `research/via-edge-image`; `viawell.edge_walls`, `edge_scan`, `image_walls`, `edge_term`, `lattice_log`, `enlarged_footprint`, the argument `c` of `well_conductance`, the walls in `viawell.build`; `checks.check_via_limits`; theory `theory.md` 2.6, validation `validation.md` 28; `tests/test_via_edge_image.py`): a via within six cells of a straight edge (the box, or an axis-parallel side of an outline) of the FVM gets `G_c = 2 pi S / (ln(r_eff/r_t) + c)` with `c = ln D_lat - ln D_true` per wall plus the diagonal image at a corner, the footprint enlarged when `c` would need a negative resistance. The via effect of a via 0.16 mm from the edge at a 1 mm cell: +14.5 % to +0.7 %, a corner +26.0 % to -1.0 %, an outline side +14.1 % to +1.1 % (inside a cell +2.5 %); bit-identical for vias out of reach (four of the five frozen boards with vias); the inverted T moves -0.0103 K at 2 mm (pending shift `via-edge-image.json`). No image within 0.6 cells of a reflex corner or of a short or slanted side, and no wall behind such a feature (`viawell.image_walls`, added by the review: the infinite mirror of the side of a T put a via 0.26 mm from its reflex corner at -13.5 % against -2.9 % without it, `validation.md` 28.1). The warning stays for the FDM, the 3D solid, a cell of 2 mm or more (where the image is applied), a via cut by the edge, outline sides that are slanted, curved or short and reflex corners.

* **Warning for a small part over a thick laminate** (`fix/small-part-laminate-warning`; `checks.check_small_part_laminate`, from `research/paper-case-studies` 16.3 and 16.4; theory `theory.md` 1.4 and 4; validation `validation.md` 21.2 and 25; tests `test_small_part_laminate_warning.py`): text only, no number moves. The FVM and FDM warn when the sub-gap `d / n` of the laminate rule, over cells the rule cuts under the part, exceeds 0.15 of the side of the part on bare laminate, or 0.10 over copper (the layered models read it too cool: -4 to -9 % bare and -6 to -14 % with a pad at 0.15 to 0.2, -12 to -24 % at 0.3 to 0.4; over a pad already about -5 % at 0.10); the side is the part's smaller side grown by the copper around it on its layer (`checks._spread_side`), so a part over a larger pad counts by the pad and a part under a plane on its layer is silent; the 3D solid warns when `d / 4` exceeds 0.15 of the same side (four slabs per gap read it up to +20 % bare / +27 % pad hot). With the defaults it fires for parts under about 2.5 mm on a 1.6 mm board and 5.2 mm on a 3.2 mm board on bare laminate, 3.8 and 7.8 mm over a pad of their size; silent on the example boards. Review: the first version used the part's side alone, fired under a top plane (error 0.02 %, the solid 0.03 %) and stayed silent over a pad of the part's size at ratios 0.10 to 0.15 (errors of 5 to 7 %); the pad limit, the copper spread and a stored-value test of the temperatures were added. The docstring and `validation.md` 25 claim that 8 slabs change the answer by under 0.6 % was wrong for small parts and is corrected; the 3.2 mm row of `validation.md` 21.2 gets a footnote (its reference was 1.6 % high).

* **Analytic Robin edge** (`feature/analytic-robin-edge`, adopts `research/analytic-edge-spatial`; `analytic.py` `_EdgeBasis`, `_edge_root`; theory `theory.md` 6; validation `validation.md` 4.1 and 13; tests `test_analytic_edge.py`). The series of the Analytic model is made of the eigenfunctions of the convective board edge (even `cos(λu)`, odd `sin(λu)`) when `h_side` is set, with one parameter `β = h_side Σ slab / Σ S` for all the layers (the perimeter of the real outline, `board.edge_perimeter`, set on the copy that `equivalent_rectangle` makes); the steady sheet, the Green-function tiles (vias, heatsinks, metal blocks) and the modal transient use the same basis, the roots are found on the pole-free forms with the asymptotes at the ends (any `β` from 1e-30 to 1e14 1/m), and without `h_side` the cosine basis is the code of before, bit for bit. It replaces the transfer of the edge loss to the faces (`fix/analytic-edge-transfer`, merged but never released). The heat through the edge is `power_out["sides"]`. A warning says when one parameter is a poor description (edge above 30 % of the loss with one layer, two layers differing by more than 1.5 times in sheet conductance or more layers by more than 2.5 times). Mean absolute error against the FVM on 135 uniform boards 0.12 % (maximum 0.32 %); the faces transfer was 3.4 % (maximum 37 %). The Lumped model is untouched. The frozen records that moved are the pending file `analytic-edge.json` (fold and delete at the 2.5.0 refreeze).

Against the 2D numerical plate the laminar form is closer at Ra 1e3 to 1e4 and on low and tall plates; vertical computed-h boards move -0.1 to -2 % of the rise on small boards and up to +0.3 % on large ones, the example board turned vertical -0.22 K (-0.36 % of the rise) in every model. `checks.check_low_rayleigh` (called once per run from `api._part_warnings`, reference model; forced-air faces excluded; horizontal boards not covered) warns below a face Rayleigh number of 1e3. The planned item on the channel Rayleigh number is dropped: the fin warning (`Ra_b < 90`) covers the channel. Tests: `test_natural_form.py`, `test_model_limit_warnings.py`. Not covered: no study between Ra 1e3 and 1e4, no 3D reference of the convection.

* **Imported copper** (`feature/imported-copper`; Auto is capped at 1 mm for imported copper, the coarse-cell warning quotes the measured numbers, `docs/validation.md` 27.3; `pcbthermal/copper.py`; theory `theory.md` 1.7; models `models.md` 19.1; validation `validation.md` 27; evidence `research/gerber-real-boards` 4.1, 10 and the study `scripts/study_program_cells.py`; tests `tests/test_imported_copper.py`). The coverage masks of an import (`Problem.imported`, one byte per pixel and layer, kept as packed text in the import record of project format 2; `model.ImportedCopper` gains `mapping`) become the copper of the board: an imported layer REPLACES the zones of that layer in every model, and the zones drawn there are ignored and warned. Three mappings (`ImportedCopper.mapping`): **faces** (default; `copper.face_conductances`, transmissibility upscaling: one local conduction problem on the pixels between two cell centres per pair of neighbours, FVM and FDM), **lambda** (the geometric mean of the Wiener bounds per cell, much faster, better on 45 degree tracks at about ten pixels per cell) and **fill** (the arithmetic fill, the old path, kept for comparison). **The faces are built on the REDUCED sheet**: the network is built from the cell fill first, so the laminate rule plans its interior laminate nodes on the real copper of each cell and moves a sheet conductance dS out of the copper node of the flagged cells; dS is then taken off every pixel of the cell, copper or bare, before the faces are computed (faces from the unreduced sheet count that laminate twice and are worse than the rule off; a test holds the order). The FVM and FDM take the faces (the FDM windows are centred on the nodes and the edge rows get half faces); the cell fill of the imported layers comes from the masks (box average, `resample_coverage`) for the vertical links, the heat capacity and every other model. The **3D solid** reads the fill (average conductivity) and warns; the **Lumped and Analytic** read only the fill and ALWAYS warn (1 to 16 % too cool in the study, 6 % on average); the FVM and FDM carry one fixed note on the accuracy (and a warning above a 1 mm cell, or below 8 pixels per cell). A cell is almost never a whole number of pixels: the face problems sample the raster at the centres of a grid at least as fine as the raster (`sample_coverage`) instead of averaging it, which keeps the clearances between copper areas (averaging bridged them and read 1.4 to 3.9 % of the rise cool on twelve real windows at cells of 0.92 and 1.09 mm; found by the review, the unaligned-cells defect); `pixels_per_cell` rounds up to an even number, at most `MAX_PIXELS_PER_CELL` = 24 (a finer raster is averaged down). **The import** (`copper.import_copper`, no GUI): rasterises the copper layers one at a time (only the packed text is kept), clears the copper outside a non-rectangular outline, turns plated round holes into vias (slots and unplated holes are said in the warnings) and refuses a panel; the default pixel is `min(m w / 4, cell / 10)` for `m` sub-pixels per pixel and `w` the thinnest feature (a quarter of the thinnest feature, and at most a tenth of the cell, so that the faces have about ten pixels per cell). **Memory** (`memory.py`): `copper_run_bytes` (the unpacked masks held, 1 byte per pixel and layer, and the working set of the face construction, 40 bytes per source pixel of the largest layer plus 50 MB; ## 2.4.3 - 2026-10-29: corrections from the investigations

* **Heater modes for the user** (after `research/gerber-real-boards/docs/heater-modes-for-the-user.md`; a `feature/` branch in the next version, 2.5.x or 2.6.0, the maintainer decides; not in this release: the margin default is 0 and there is no margin feature yet). In step 2 a "Heat source" list with only the modes valid for the part: QFN, DFN: exposed pad (proposed), exposed pad from the footprint name, package envelope, hull, box; SOT-223: tab (proposed), hull, envelope, box; every other part: hull (proposed), box, envelope. A **Margin** box of 0 to 1 mm, default 0 (a margin reads cooler: 0 to -22 % on the exposed pads, `validation.md` 29.4; it is not a safety margin), and "apply to all parts with this footprint". Pieces: `parts.grow_polygon`, `shape_kinds`, kinds `envelope` and `pad_name`, `edits["shape_margin_mm"]`, no format change; the re-import must keep kind and margin (the delicate place). **No library of exposed-pad sizes** (25 to 30 datasheet entries, sizes differ between makers; the paste windows are on the safe side of the pad by 0.6 to 8.2 %). The shape "largest opening" is left as it is for now (right only for a single tab, +22 to +299 % otherwise; the study proposes to stop offering it). Effort 2 to 3 days, low risk; frozen numbers unchanged (imported parts only, margin 0).
* **Size of the models**: multigrid (`pyamg`, only above about 500 000 unknowns; optional); a hard limit for FVM and FDM if the guard does not cover them.
* **Metal block**: the capacity of the fins (a time simulation of a finned block responds too fast), fin end faces (0.7 to 0.9 % of the rise), adjacent blocks, a cell shared by two blocks, time simulation with a block in Lumped and Analytic. Evidence: `research/block-and-fins/INTEGRATION.md`.
* **Time and pulses**: the top path in the time simulation, a Foster chain / heat capacity of the junction and the heatsink with `dTj = Zth(tp, D) P` as a check; the pad-size effect of Lumped and Analytic near the edge of a cut board (`research/outline/README.md` 4.2). Evidence: `research.md` E3 to E5, R2.
* **Heatsinks and the part**: other air speed as an estimated factor of the natural value, flow linked to the board air speed, heatsinks with a fan, scenarios (`theta_sa` scaling with the rise, orientation, geometry with the channel effect), the junction node in the FDM, Lumped and Analytic, the heatsink as a plate layer with an interface of its own area, clearer names and live Tj for the source/component heater, passive areas and a component library (LEDs, processors with lid and paste). Evidence: `research.md` E3 to E8, R2 to R9.
* **Calculator**: a virtual JEDEC test to predict θJA, ΨJT, ΨJB and recover a missing package resistance (and fix the bottom layer of the JEDEC 2s2p board); the known-power reference (+-5 % with +-1 K reading error against -19 to -27 % without). Evidence: `docs/calculators.md` 2.1 and 2.4.
* **Import**: Gerber and Excellon copper per layer (rasterised fill fraction; the point-via fix of 2.4.5 lets the cell go below 0.5 mm), the `copper_mapping` option (`research/gerber-models/INTEGRATION.md`), KiCad `.kicad_pcb`, a power list from a CSV or BOM by designator, orthotropic copper from the raster. Evidence: `research/gerber-import`, `gerber-models`, `gerber-nets`. The 2.5.0 design and branch list are item 2 of 'Next, in order'. **When the Gerber import is implemented** (maintainer's wish): also read the other layers, such as the solder paste, so that a part can be drawn by clicking the paste polygon of its pads and giving it a power (the heater footprint comes from the file); the missing piece is which part is which, that is its reference designator, to be read from a pick-and-place (centroid) file or from ODB++ or IPC-2581 when the project has one, and typed by hand otherwise; first find a real project that holds this data to try the import on, and run the studies of `research/gerber-import` and `research/gerber-nets` again in the research environment before and after (item E11 of `research/ROADMAP.md`).
* **Board, stack-up and outline**: solder mask above and below the copper, a material per copper layer, a library of fabricator stack-ups; arc and spline outlines, holes and cut-outs, a warning for a neck narrower than three cells, the Lumped network on any outline; the disclaimer (a design aid, to be validated by measurement) also in every report. A heatsink on the laminate side of a one-layer board stays unsupported.

### Ideas taken from similar projects (all features, in the order of value for the effort)

Read as web summaries (KiCad plug-ins such as KiCad_Thermal_Sim, pcb-sim-toolkit, kicad-electrothermal-sim, KiPIDA; commercial tools; FYPA, padne and altium_monkey, which are AGPL-3.0: ideas and public formats only, no code copied).

1. Electro-thermal coupling: Joule heating of the copper (`Problem.electrical`), IPC-2152 trace-rise check, copper resistivity with temperature, by the existing fixed-point loop.
2. Thermal via tools: an array generator, the resistance of an array against the 4 / 16 via figures of the fabricator guides, a via-count sweep.
3. Parameter sweeps and comparison of two designs with plots and an A/B table.
4. Pass/fail checks with an exit code (`--fail-on fail`), an HTML or PDF report, a result cache.
5. Regulator and converter losses from efficiency in the Power Calculator.
6. Parameters read from the CAD symbol fields, our settings in a sidecar file.
7. Probes and pin tables, export for ParaView and for an external 3D check (STEP/XAO or a mesh).
8. Cooling presets and a closed box with an internal air rise (no CFD).
9. Local mesh refinement or a triangular mesh; release automation (CI, standalone program on a tag).
